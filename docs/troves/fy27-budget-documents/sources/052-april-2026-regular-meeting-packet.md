March 23, 2026
The Board members assembled at 6 p.m. after an Executive Session pursuant to 1 M.R.S.A. §405 (6) (A) to discuss

Members present for the Board Workshop: Ms. DeAngelis, Mr. Feller, Ms. Holman, Ms. Richardson, Mr. Risch, Mr.
Smith and Student Representatives Alex Davison and Angela Kabisa (Not present: Mr. Dowling)
Workshop
Abigail Ketchen, Director of Finance, provided an overview of the current FY27 budget proposal, which upholds the
district’s mission, maintains class sizes within School Board limits, and creates a path to financial sustainability. She
also reviewed some factors that led to the current budget situation, including staff, enrollment and funding
misalignment, the absence of a formal fund balance policy, and leadership instability.
In the proposed budget, there is $1.61 million in non-personnel and other reductions from the FY27 version for all
departments’ budget requests.
Dr. Jo Prince, Assistant Superintendent, provided an overview of the elementary proposal. It currently does not use
local dollars to expand the PreK program in 2026-27. It also recommends closing a school, but would not
decommission it. If a school closure does not happen, it is estimated that an additional 12-16 positions from across
the district would need to be eliminated to create a balanced budget. There was consideration to look at all five
elementary school buildings for a physical plant analysis, and the recommendation is to close Kaler.
The options being presented for configuration include:
Option A: Primary and Intermediate Model (PreK-1 at Dyer and Small, Grades 2-4 at Brown and Skillin)
Option B: Full Grade Band Model (Brown PreK-4, Dyer K-4, Skillin K-4, Small PreK-4)
Association leaders reviewed the impact lists, sought clarity, and ensured the contract was upheld. A joint letter from
the four Associations was sent on 3/15 to share that they were consulted on the process and 3/16 the district shared
the timeline for notification. Staff who were impacted by the budget proposal were notified on 3/18 and
conversations were offered on 3/19 and 3/20.
With Ellyn Feerick, Director of Curriculum, Instruction, and Assessment, resigning from the role, the district will
use a new Curriculum Council model.
The Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion position is under review to be changed from a director role to a coordinator or
strategist position.
Rebecca Stern, SPMS Principal, shared some big picture proposed shifts and reductions that are shared between the
middle school and high school.
Sarah Glenn, SPHS Principal, shared proposed position reductions as well as structural and services changes to the
high school.

Budget Update
Dr. Jo Prince provided an overview of the elementary transition planning. This proposed new vision for elementary
schools in South Portland begins with a shared vision grounded in student learning There will be stakeholder
meetings at each school to allow for input from staff, families, and the community. There will also be survey
opportunities and a committee created to move this work forward. The process is underway with logistics for
transportation routes and for teachers who will be moving.
Director and administrator changes to the FY27 proposed budget includes the reduction of a Diversity, Equity, and
Inclusion Director and the second Assistant Director of Special Education.
The proposed budget tax impact would be an annual increase of $257 for a South Portland home valued at $514,000
for the school portion.
If the Board does not pass the three motions on the agenda, there will be a Board meeting on Thursday, April 2 in
the SPHS Lecture Hall at 6 p.m. If the Board passes all three motions, the Board’s Budget will be presented to City
Council on April 7, 2026.

Good evening,
Despite all the many events and learning experiences happening across the district, the South
Portland School Department continues to see strong engagement, achievement, and positive
school communities.
At Brown School, their spring season of Girls on the Run is underway with 22 students and 5
dedicated coaches. They are grateful for the staff members who volunteer their time to support
this program, which promotes confidence, teamwork, and healthy habits. Students are also
benefiting from learning beyond the classroom, including a recent first-grade field trip to the
planetarium as part of an interdisciplinary unit. They are also looking forward to an upcoming
author visit from Raul III for all elementary grade levels. In addition, Brown School classrooms
have earned over 300 “golden tickets” for demonstrating safe, respectful, and responsible
behavior, celebrating together with class rewards that help strengthen a positive school culture.
At Dyer Elementary School, students recently completed their Read-A-Thon with nearly 200,000
minutes read, reflecting a strong commitment to literacy across the school. They are now
preparing for their upcoming Character Parade, where students will dress as book characters
alongside their classmates, with families invited to attend this celebration.
At Kaler Elementary School, students in grades 2–4 recently welcomed Crusher, the Maine
Celtics mascot, along with his trainer. Students participated in engaging activities focused on
healthy lifestyles, teamwork, and basketball skills, including jump shots and layups, in a fun and
interactive school experience.
At Skillin Elementary School, students participated in a strong month of literacy-focused
activities, including weekly “Drop Everything and Read” sessions and a mystery reader event
featuring surprise guests. They also hosted a successful Family Literacy and Math Night with
hands-on activities for students and families. The month concluded with a community
celebration honoring Principal Connolly, who received the Maine Association of School Libraries
Administrator of the Year Award.

celebrated with a schoolwide assembly and dance party. The celebration included students
honoring teachers in creative ways such as pie-in-the-face, coloring hair, and gift wrapping.

confidence, fitness, and friendships through structured, goal-oriented activities.
At South Portland Middle School, students and staff are looking forward to several upcoming
spring events, including the Staff Talent Show on April 30th and World Cultures Day on May 6th.
Staff continue to focus on collaborative instructional planning and engaging students in
meaningful, hands-on learning experiences.
Lastly, at South Portland High School, students recently performed the musical Chicago,
showcasing strong talent and dedication. They have also held family-teacher conferences,

11th-grade science testing, and a college information night. This week, they are hosting
Community Week, which celebrates the diverse identities, cultures, and experiences within the
school community through spirit days, performances, athletics, guest speakers, and wellness
activities.

Temperature Standards for School Facilities
The South Portland School Department is committed to maintaining safe, healthy, and
comfortable learning and working environments for staff and students. These guidelines are
established in alignment with the Maine L.D. 1: An Act to Improve Public Health Regarding
Indoor Air Quality in Schools.
Optimal Indoor Environment: The Board believes that the optimal indoor environment for
teaching and learning is between a minimum of 65 degrees and a maximum of 80 degrees and
adopts this as the “temperature standard” for the District’s schools.
The facilities department will maintain updated procedures to assist schools in mitigating
temperature changes outside of the optimal environment.
Decision Thresholds:
When temperatures are predicted to be outside of the optimal environment, administrators (or
their delegates) shall monitor both indoor and outdoor temperatures.
● Outdoor Recess/Gym Class: School administrators should decide whether or not to
have outdoor recess or gym due to temperature concerns.
● Early Dismissal/School Cancellation: The Superintendent will determine if school is
canceled or dismissed early due to temperature concerns.
● Co-curricular Events: The Superintendent will determine if co-curricular events are
canceled due to temperature concerns.

Procedure for Exceeding the Maximum Comfort Zone
When temperatures in the building exceed the maximum comfort zone, staff will follow
these procedures when safe, allowable, and available (but not limited to):
● Opening windows
● Pulling down shades or blinds
● Using fans
● Opening doors
● Turning off heat-producing electronics/equipment
● Having water readily available
● Moving classrooms to cooler spaces
Procedure for Falling Below the Minimum Comfort Zone
When temperatures in the building fall below the minimum comfort zone, staff will
follow these procedures when safe, allowable, and available (but not limited to):
● Incorporate more opportunities for physical activity
● Consider having extra layers of clothing available
Procedures for Facilities Department
The Director of Buildings and Grounds, will implement measures to mitigate high
temperatures in schools.
● The Facilities Department will take inventory of air conditioning units and fans at
each school and ensure they are in good working order.
● School administrators are responsible for deciding where to place the air
conditioning units and fans within their buildings.
● The custodial staff will move the equipment as directed.
● Temperature conditions will be evaluated using a central reading at ~5 feet above
the floor in a closed zone, Consistent with ASHRAE 55. This provides an
objective and consistent standard of record, recognizing that localized hot or cold
spots may exist near windows, doors, or vents.
