### PROCLAMATION - Designating May 18-24th as Arbor Week in the City of South Portland. Passage Requires Majority Vote.
POSITION PAPER OF THE CITY MANAGER
Attached is a proclamation recognizing May 18 – 24, 2025 as Arbor Week within the City of South Portland. As noted in the attached memo from Parks and Recreation Director Karl Coughlin, passage of this resolution is one requirement for maintaining our Tree City USA status, which we have received for 46 years.
The four criteria to becoming a Tree City USA community include: maintaining a tree board or department, having a community tree ordinance, spending at least $2 per capita on urban forestry, and celebrating Arbor Day. There are only 18 other communities in Maine who hold this status, and South Portland has maintained this distinction longer than 14 of those other communities. This is a testament to the value our community places on trees and the investments we have made in that pursuit over the past several decades.

### ORDER #205-25/26 - Amending Texas Instruments TIF Development Program to Extend the District and reestablish it as a new Transit-Oriented Growth & Infrastructure TIF Development District. Passage Requires Majority Vote.
The City of South Portland has received a request from Texas Instruments (TI) to extend the TI (formerly National Semiconductor) Tax Increment Financing (TIF) District before its anticipated expiration on June 30, 2026. This request is being made in collaboration with City staff, who have independently reviewed potential benefits of such an extension to financially support a variety of City projects in future years.
The TI TIF district is proposed to become the Transit-Oriented Growth and Infrastructure (TOGI) TIF district, and will be extended through June 30, 2046. Historically, the maximum term of a TIF District was 30 years, but a recent change in State Law now allows certain districts to be extended an additional 20 years, albeit with restrictions on what those TIF revenues can be spent on, which primarily includes transit-oriented development and/or affordable housing. 25% of the TIF revenues can be spent on credit enhancement agreements (CEAs) and TI is seeking such an arrangement with the City.
The City Council heard the request and staff presentation at a workshop held on February 3, 2026 and expressed support for this item moving to a Public Hearing, with several recommended changes made to the boundaries of the district and which parcels should be included. Since that time staff have further refined the TIF District area, which focuses primarily in the Maine Mall and Cash Corner areas of the City, as well as along the transit corridors throughout the City.
Some of the proposed district parcels are already in TIF districts, including the TI TIF, Transit-Oriented Development TIF, and Growth Area TIF districts. The proposal is to partially consolidate some of those parcels into the newly formed TOGI TIF district, which necessitates sunsetting the other two districts. Those related public hearings and Council actions follow this item on the agenda. In effect, this will allow the City to leverage the significant TIF revenues generated by Texas Instruments for a range of necessary and meaningful capital projects and other uses identified by the City as high priority and which would enable transit-oriented growth and development.
The attached staff memo provides more detail on the proposal, including financial projections and examples of eligible project costs. Required public notice was published in the Press Herald on Friday, April 24, 2026 and is attached.
TIF Legal Counsel Phil Saucier from Bernstein Shur will present this proposal. Judith Borelli and Matthew Beck of Texas Instruments, and Economic Development Director Lea Duffy and Assistant City Manager Joshua Reny will be present to answer questions.

### ORDER #206-25/26 - Terminating the Transit-Oriented Tax Incrementing Financing District and Development Program. Passage Requires Majority Vote.
This item is a companion order to ORDER #205-25/26.

The Transit-Oriented Tax Increment Financing (TIF) district was established in 2010 with an original term of 30 years. It includes several prospective development parcels throughout the City, many of which have not been developed during the past 15+ years. The district generates approximately $36k per year in TIF revenues, significantly less than the TIF revenues that will be generated by the reestablished TOGI TIF district.

This order will terminate the existing Transit-Oriented TIF district so that several parcels in the Cash Corner neighborhood can be absorbed within the larger Transit-Oriented Growth and Infrastructure (TOGI) TIF noted in the prior agenda item.

TIF Legal Counsel Phil Saucier from Bernstein Shur, Economic Development Director Lea Duffy, and Assistant City Manager Joshua Reny will be present to answer questions.

### ORDER #208-25/26 - Transferring Up To $2 Million to the School Department from the City's Unassigned Fund Balance to Cover an Anticipated Budget Deficit for Fiscal Year 2026. Passage Requires Majority Vote.
POSITION PAPER OF THE CITY MANAGER
During workshop discussions on April 14 and 28, the City Council agreed to forgive up to $2 million in budget overages anticipated by the School Department to close out fiscal year 2026. This overage will be absorbed by the City's accrued unassigned fund balance. After overspending their approved budget amounts the previous two fiscal years, the School Department has utilized almost all of its own fund balance, and anticipates it will have exhausted all remaining fund balance and accrued a deficit of up to $2 million by June 30, 2026 when this fiscal year ends.

The attached Order codifies the Council's workshop guidance, whereby the City will transfer up to $2 million from its fund balance to the School Department to cover the anticipated overage. If the deficit is less, the City will only transfer the lesser amount. If the deficit is more than $2 million, the School Department will need to repay that overage back to the City.

As a reminder, transferring these funds to the School Department will mean that the City will have up to $2 million less in FY28 to pay for one-time operating budget costs (like we did for firefighter PFAS-free foam), buying down the tax rate, and capital budget items, as most capital items are paid for with excess unassigned fund balance.

As noted in the FY27 CIP, the projections for FY28 - after factoring out facilities costs because staff are assuming the public safety bond will pass - there are over $5 million in needed CIP items that will rely on fund balance. However, the City will only have $2,074,773, plus whatever we add to it at the end of this fiscal year. If the bond does not pass, we will add hundreds of thousands (if not over a million) in additional expenses to make needed initial repairs to Police and Central Fire facilities. Without adequate fund balance, the Council will either need to forego certain capital items, or raise taxes on the City's share of the tax rate to pay for them.

Finance Director Ellen Sanborn will be present should Council have further questions about this item.

### ORDER #157-25/26 - Amending Order #135-24/25 to Update the Charge of the Mahoney City Center Committee. Postponed from March 19, 2026. Passage Requires Majority Vote.
POSITION PAPER OF THE CITY MANAGER
NOTE: This item was postponed from the March 3, 2026, March 19, 2026 , and April 7, 2026 Council meetings.

On February 18, 2025, the City Council approved an Order that created the Mahoney City Center Committee (M3C). Following recommendations from the Mahoney City Center Committee and subsequent Council discussion during a February 10, 2026 workshop, guidance was given to staff and the design team to exclude the Library from the project, and to find a location for the Police Station other than the Mahoney property.

This Order serves to formally remove the Library from the Mahoney project and purview of the M3C, and to remove references to the Police Station. The Order has also been amended to remove reference to presenting Council with a "70% design" in July and instead replaces it with "schematic design". This is less than what a 70% design would be, but is less costly and still able to produce an accurate cost amount to present to voters. The original Order is attached for your reference.

Update (March 31, 2026): Over the past two meetings, the Council has changed its position on the Mahoney project. Council is now interested in bringing the Library back into the project, and has asked the Mahoney City Center Committee to explore public/private partnerships to help fund the project. In addition, Council has pushed the date of a potential referendum to November 2027, and has given guidance to staff to hold a November 2026 referendum for a new Police and Central Fire station.

Based on this new guidance, staff is proposing an alternative Order #157-25/26. This Order keeps some of the prior changes and makes new ones given recent developments. See attached.

Please note two specific changes: 1) the removal of the Police Chief and Fire Chief from the M3C, and 2) authorizing the committee, as part of their review of public/private partnership options, to consider scenarios that do not include all services within Mahoney, as some of that space may be needed to be provided to a developer in order for them to enter into a partnership. (Attached also is a red-lined version of the original order showing these alternative changes).
