### Resignation of School Board Member Adrian Dowling (District Five)
POSITION PAPER OF THE CITY CLERK

On April 6, 2026, the City Clerk was in receipt of notice of Adrian Dowling's resignation from the School Board (District 5). Mr. Dowling's term expires December, 2027.

 

The City Charter outlines the process for vacancies on the School Board:

[Sec.] 904. Vacancy.

Whenever a vacancy in the school board occurs the vacancy may be filled by a majority vote of all the members of the city council and the member so elected shall serve until the next regular municipal election when a new member shall be elected to fill the vacancy for the unexpired term, if any.

Because of the term “may”, it is permissive, in that the Council may choose to fill the vacancy or may choose to leave it vacant until the next election (in November, and petition papers would be available late July). It’s important to note that if the Council is to appoint someone to fill the vacancy, the person’s term expires when whoever is elected in November fills the seat (if the appointee is interested in running, they will still need to take out petition papers and qualify for the ballot).

The School Board has been notified of Mr. Dowling's resignation.

Should the Council proceed with filling the vacancy, the City Clerk will immediately begin to post public notice for applicants to be brought forward for consideration and appointment.

Mr. Dowling Resignation

### ORDINANCE #20-25/26 - Amending Chapter 4, "Bicycles" of the Code of Ordinances to Update the Definition of a Bicycle and Add a Definition of Electric Bicycle to Better Align with State Definitions. Second Reading. First Reading Approved on April 7, 2026. Passage Requires Majority Vote. ROLL CALL VOTE.
POSITION PAPER OF THE CITY MANAGER

On March 10, 2026, City Council held a workshop at the request of the Bicycle & Pedestrian Advisory Committee to allow electronic bicycles on City property.

 

As noted during the workshop, based in part on guidance from the City's legal counsel that City Council should expressly state whether electronic bicycles (e-bikes) are allowed on the City's Greenbelt (current interpretation is that they are not), on April 18, 2023, the City Council passed first reading of an ordinance amendment to Chapter 18 that would have specifically allowed them.

 

However, on May 2, 2023, the Council voted to withdraw the item from the agenda without prejudice due to concerns raised by Councilor West about the width of some portions of the Greenbelt not meeting the American Association of State Highway and Transportation Officials (AASHTO) standard of 10 feet minimum. As such, e-bikes remained prohibited (although in practice, South Portland does not actively patrol the Greenbelt for e-bike enforcement).

 

Since that time, the City has conducted an in-house assessment of the Greenbelt to identify constraints and compile all existing easements. Public Works concluded that although some areas are close to the minimum width requirements, a full replacement of the entire Greenbelt should occur due to erosion, tree root issues, and pavement damage throughout the corridor. The next steps would be to secure funding for an inspection of the Anthoine Bridge and to begin preliminary design work. This work is estimated to cost approximately $200,000. Alternatively, funding could be pursued for the entire project, including construction, which is currently estimated at $6.5 million.

 

On October 14, 2025, the Bike Ped Committee once again requested a workshop on this topic (see attached). Based on input from Councilor West, at the March workshop City staff presented an alternative to what Council considered back in 2023. Staff are proposing to essentially align the City's definitions of a bicycle with the State's definition. Under the State's definition, Class 1 and Class 2 e-bikes are akin to traditional foot-powered bicycles, and thus would be allowed on the Greenbelt (and other City properties where pedal bicycles are allowed). Achieving this would require amendments to Chapters 4, 15, and 18 of the City's Code of Ordinances.

 

At the workshop, a majority of Councilors gave guidance to proceed with the necessary ordinance amendments. Attached is the first of three separate ordinance changes needed to better align City definitions with the State's, and effectively allow Class 1 and 2 e-bikes on City property such as the Greenbelt.

 

First reading of this ordinance passed on April 7, 2026. If the second reading is passed this evening the ordinance will be in effect in twenty days.

 

Parks & Recreation Director Karl Coughlin will be present for this discussion.

### ORDER #187-25/26 - Adopting the South Portland Comprehensive Plan. Passage Requires Majority Vote. ROLL CALL VOTE.
POSITION PAPER OF THE CITY MANAGER

The Comprehensive Plan Committee (CPC) has completed its work and voted to forward a final draft of the updated Comprehensive Plan to the City Council with a recommendation for adoption. See attached.

 

The plan reflects:

         Extensive staff review and technical refinement

         State agency coordination and preliminary certification under the Growth Management Act

         Significant public engagement, including over 600 comments

         Council guidance from prior workshops, particularly regarding the Eastern Waterfront

 

The document establishes long-term policy direction for land use, housing, transportation, economic development, and resilience.

 

As noted in the attached memo from Planning Director Milan Nevajda, while the plan is comprehensive, several major themes are central to Council consideration:

         Managed Growth & Housing: Increased density and mixed-use development along corridors and targeted growth areas

         Eastern Waterfront Strategy: Refined land use framework balancing economic activity, public access, and environmental constraints

         Flexibility in Implementation: Policies allowing Council discretion on civic facilities, open space tradeoffs, and future regulatory tools

         Resilience & Infrastructure: Emphasis on flood resilience, multimodal transportation, and coordinated regional planning

 

The Council is being asked to take formal action on adoption of the Comprehensive Plan, which will:

         Establish official City policy guiding future land use and development decisions

         Enable continued State certification and access to related benefits

         Direct future ordinance changes and capital planning

 

The Council has the following four options tonight:

- Adopt as Presented- Approve the plan in its current form
- Advances implementation immediately

 

- Adopt with Clear Revisions- Make targeted amendments at the time of adoption
- Appropriate for discrete, well-defined changes

 

- Continue for Substantial Amendments- Delay adoption to allow for additional revisions and review
- Suitable if Council seeks to evaluate new or modified policy language

 

- Remand to CPC with Guidance- Send the plan back to the Comprehensive Plan Committee
- Requires clear direction on specific policy issues needing further work

 

The plan has undergone extensive review and already received State consistency determination (subject to final recertification). Further substantial changes may delay implementation and require additional review cycles. Adoption does not finalize all regulatory details; many policies will be implemented through future ordinances and Council actions. It is important to note that while future Council decisions related to land use in particular cannot run in contravention to the plan, the plan itself does not override existing ordinances, nor does it require Council to adopt all of the recommendations.

 

Although there are some areas of the document where staff disagree and have shared those concerns with the Planning staff, we also respect the extensive process that has culminated with this plan, which I suspect has been the longest comprehensive planning process in Maine history. Despite these concerns, I recommend Council approval.

 

If Council adopts the plan, it will be submitted to the State for final certification. Staff will begin implementation through ordinance updates and strategic initiatives, and will work with the CPC to come up with a workplan for short, medium, and long term priorities.

 

CPC Chair Howard Kunin and Planning Director Milan Nevajda will be present to provide an overview of the plan to the Council. My gratitude to the committee members who have spent years painstakingly working on this plan, and to the many members of the public who have provided feedback throughout the process.
