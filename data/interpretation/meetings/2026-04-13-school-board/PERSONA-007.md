### Reactions

The thing that I'm still thinking about at 11 PM is that SPTA time-beyond-contract number. We sat through two hours of a meeting and voted on a city ask, and nobody can tell us what the actual FY26 exposure is because the submission process just rolled out last week. That notation has apparently been sitting in yellow on the finance committee's monthly report. I don't want to second-guess Holman for catching it -- she did the right thing surfacing it publicly -- but the fact that we're voting on what to ask the city council for tomorrow night without knowing what our current-year books actually close at is a real problem. The range we do have, $80K to $1.1M, is so wide it's almost not actionable. And the TBC is sitting on top of it. We will not have a clean number until May at the earliest.

The Abigail location-based analysis was genuinely useful, and I'm glad she did it. The $400K facilities cut being misread as the full cost of Kaylor has been creating confusion in community conversations for weeks. The actual number -- approximately $4M, half of all FY27 reductions -- is something people can reason about. And when she was asked directly whether there's a path to keeping Kaylor open, she said no. That's hard to hear, but I respect that she said it clearly and on the record. What I'm less settled on is the $360K city ask. I understand why we're making it, and I support opening that conversation. But SROs are a service we've always paid for, so that piece is genuinely new ground, and I don't think we can count on it. The $540K from a 1% increase going into contingency is the more durable ask -- it rebuilds something structural. I just hope the council doesn't hear both numbers as one combined request and decide we're overreaching.

Three more resignations tonight, including Beth Kellogg at Brown. That's a principal opening mid-cycle, which is a different hiring problem than a classroom teacher. The recall list covers the teachers, which is the right process. But a principal search in this environment, with everything Kaylor families are going through, with the city council meeting tomorrow and April 29 coming fast -- the administrative bandwidth question is real. And Abigail flagged FY28 in plain language: careful planning required. She's not predicting a repeat of this year, but she's also not ruling it out. I've been on boards long enough to know that "careful planning required" from your finance director in April, thirteen months before a budget is due, means you do not take any of these FY27 decisions as the end of the work. They're the beginning of the next round.
