### Reactions

Okay so I ended up watching the city council meeting last night — well, parts of it — because someone in my mommy Facebook group was freaking out about it. Most of it is honestly four-plus hours of oil tanks and condos and whether to allow six-story buildings near Bug Light, and nobody could agree on anything. But there were a few moments early on that I can't stop thinking about.

Right at the start, like five minutes in, they breezed past this announcement that a school board member just quit. District 5, Adrian Dowling, resigned in early April. And instead of appointing someone, they're just putting it on the November ballot. So there's a vacant school board seat from now until November while they're in the middle of deciding whether to cut 42 teachers. Who is actually representing those families right now?? And then I caught something in the consent calendar — they set polling times for a school budget referendum on June 9. As in, residents literally vote on whether the school budget passes. I had zero idea that was a thing. Nobody explains what happens if it fails. It just went by.

The rest of the night was mostly the waterfront zoning stuff, which I could barely follow. But at one point a business group representative got up and explicitly said the school cuts are happening because the tax base isn't big enough, and the answer is new development. Which... I mean, maybe? Except the city planner also basically explained that between planning, zoning, approvals, and construction, it takes years and years before any development actually generates tax money. So the schools keep cutting now while we wait for condos that might not exist until Mia is already in second grade. One more thing I can't get out of my head: one of the councilors mentioned Kaylor School in passing — something about how tank emissions wouldn't be good for it "while it's still open." Just threw it out there. Nobody followed up. I don't even know where Kaylor is, but I'm looking it up now.
