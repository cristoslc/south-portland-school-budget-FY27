### Reactions

So the school presentation got bumped to next week, which means I drove to City Hall — or watched the recording — for a budget meeting and the main event didn't happen. Fine. But here's the thing I'll be chewing on all week: buried in the middle of Morelli's fund balance section, around the 20-minute mark, he mentions almost in passing that the city has set aside $3 million as an anticipated loan to the school because the school is going to run out of its own fund balance *this year*. Not FY27. This year. About two million in operating overage, another million in food service. Everyone's been writing about the FY27 crisis — 78 positions, $7.2 million gap, 12% of staff — and meanwhile the district is already in the red on the current budget, and the city is quietly planning to cover it from savings. That's the story nobody's filed yet, and I want to call Ellen Sanborn tomorrow to understand exactly what triggered those overages.

The tax breakdown math is the other thing I'll use. Morelli put it in plain English in a way the district never does: sixty-one cents of every dollar you pay in property taxes goes to the schools. For the average South Portland home, that's four grand a year, out of a sixty-five hundred dollar annual bill. The city's share of this year's 5.1% overall increase? One percentage point of impact. The school's share? Three-point-six. That's the lede for next week's piece, assuming I can get the actual school presentation on April 14. What I still don't have is any school-board-approved number — Morelli was working off March 23 figures and explicitly said the board hasn't voted yet. So every number I'm looking at is provisional.

The Flock Safety thing was unexpected and ran long. Four residents came in with specific concerns — ICE access, a five-month ACLU FOA request that apparently hasn't been processed yet because the check from the requester hadn't cleared, and a possible conflict with Maine's 21-day data retention law versus a contract apparently specifying 30 days. The chief was engaged and transparent, offered to let anyone see the audit logs, and I believe he believes in the tool. But there's a real question I want to follow up on: the city manager said that grant-funded cameras *do* appear on council agendas, but the residents' concern was specifically about the FOA delay and the contract terms. I want to read that contract. This is probably a sidebar to the school story, but given the community anxiety right now about immigration enforcement, it's not a nothing story.
