### Reactions

So the school budget wasn't even there last night — the board still hasn't voted, so they just pushed it to the 14th. Which means the one number I actually went to hear got deferred. The city manager did present his side of the budget, and honestly the municipal piece looks pretty well-managed. They held the city's share to 3%, which is below their own guidance, and the five-metric tax comparison was actually reassuring — we're the lowest of our abutting peers in how much the tax commitment has grown since 2018. That's a real data point, not spin.

But the thing that jumped out at me — buried in the middle of the city presentation — was the $3 million the finance director has already set aside expecting the *school* to overshoot its budget this year. Two million in operating overages and a million in food service. That's not in the school's fund balance anymore because the school's fund balance is basically gone. So the city is now carrying the school's cash flow risk inside its own reserves. The city's fund balance just barely clears the 9-12% policy minimum after that reservation. If the school overage is worse than estimated, or if the council increases the school budget for FY27, that buffer gets thinner fast.

The rest of the meeting was mostly Flock camera questions from the public — the police chief answered pretty thoroughly, and to be fair, the audit log and 21-day retention answers were substantive. But it's a $21,500 annual licensing cost. Meanwhile the structural school budget story — enrollment down 23% in four years, staffing up 82 positions, per-pupil cost highest in the comparison set — that whole conversation happens April 14. I'll be back for that one.
