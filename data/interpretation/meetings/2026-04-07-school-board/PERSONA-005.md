### Structured Points

#### 1. State funds for vulnerable students treated as general contingency
- **Fact:** The district expects approximately $152,620 for low-SES students and $150,000 for McKinney-Vento homeless students from a potential state emergency appropriation. The administration's proposal routes these funds into one-year teacher positions and general contingency. Board Member Feller explicitly flagged that directing dollars designated for "unhoused and marginalized students" into contingency "doesn't make a lot of sense," and the question of whether the district felt a responsibility to spend these funds in those communities received only a vague response from Dr. Prince.

#### 2. Behavior strategist role eliminated rather than restored
- **Fact:** The district is not recalling the existing behavior strategist (a social worker who bridges general ed and special ed, supporting students before they receive an IEP) but is instead posting a renamed BCBA-credentialed role. Member Richardson noted that the district has also RIF'd two other social workers, and said directly: "The priority of mental health seems to be declining for this district." The administration's rationale was that BCBA training is more rigorous -- but the person with demonstrated community impact and a social work background would not be eligible for recall under the new posting.

#### 3. Embedded OT model dismantled in functional life skills classrooms

### Reactions

So I sat through nearly four hours of that meeting, and here's what I keep coming back to: they have three hundred thousand dollars coming from the state -- money that is tied, at least in spirit, to low-income students and homeless kids -- and the plan is to park most of it in contingency. Member Feller actually said out loud that it "doesn't make a lot of sense" to put funds for unhoused students into a general buffer, and the assistant superintendent's answer was basically, well, if there are no restrictions, it goes where we need it. That is exactly the move we always see. The equity language is in the grant description. The equity language disappears in the allocation. Nobody on that board demanded a firm answer on whether those dollars would touch a single McKinney-Vento student's life differently.

And then there's the functional life skills situation, which honestly shook me. An ed tech got up and described his classroom being short-staffed for nearly the entire school year. The FLS teacher at Dyer came from home in Westbrook because she felt compelled to say on record: we cannot attract ed techs, these OT positions are not replaceable with ed techs, and I have taken a real lunch break maybe twice in fourteen years because I don't leave those kids unsupervised for safety reasons. The superintendent's response? Safety concerns haven't "risen" to his level. Those are students with significant disabilities. That's the population we're supposed to be most legally and morally obligated to protect, and the administration is replacing specialized OT support with a position category they freely admit they cannot fill. That's not a budget tradeoff. That's a service delivery failure for the kids with the highest need.

The moment that actually gave me something to hold onto was Jamie Watson naming the bus stop inequity. She said it plainly: there are more stops on the west side of the city, which skews higher income, and fewer in Red Bank, which doesn't. She's been advocating for those stops and hasn't gotten traction. And then Claire Holman said something I didn't expect -- that any community vote on reconfiguration would miss a big chunk of affected parents because a lot of them can't vote, and they tend to be "where we have the fewer bus stops." She named it. It was brief, it was almost parenthetical, but she made the connection between immigrant families and transportation access and political voice in about ten seconds. I want more of that. I want the board to build policy from that observation instead of just noting it. Because right now, the budget that just passed 4-2 has no mechanism for any of it -- no equity reporting, no resource equity audit for high-need schools, no demographic transparency. Those were mandated in 2024. They still don't exist.
