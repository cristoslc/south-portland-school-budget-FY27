# Cumulative Summary: Lila (PERSONA-014)

### Current Understanding

As of April 14, Lila is a fourth grader at Dyer Elementary approaching the end of her last year before middle school. She has followed the South Portland school budget crisis since early December, when she first encountered a $1.9 million shortfall and a spending freeze preventing teachers from buying classroom supplies. That deficit has since grown to a stated $8.4 million. The district's reserves are depleted, state funding covers roughly 20% of per-pupil costs against an expected 55%, and elementary enrollment has dropped by 300 students over four years.

Seventy-two school employees received layoff notices in mid-March, including 42 teachers, though two positions were added back in April. Nine formal reconfiguration scenarios were presented in early March, including one listing Dyer as "CLOSED." The board subsequently voted 6–1 to close Kaler Elementary and 4–2 to redesignate Dyer as a Pre-K through grade 1 school. Both votes occurred March 30. On April 13, the board chair announced strong support for an April 29 vote to halt reconfiguration for September 2026; that vote has not yet taken place.

The FY27 budget passed the board 4–2 on April 7 and was presented to city council on April 14 — one week late. A community referendum is set for June 9. State law prevents the city council from directing which specific buildings to close; it may only set a bottom-line dollar amount. Lila now understands this legal split as the structural reason the school board and city council have been pointing at each other since February.

Two Dyer teachers have resigned since the reconfiguration process began. Dyer's functional life skills classroom was short-staffed with safety concerns throughout the current school year — something Lila did not learn until April 7. A recall petition has been filed against the board chair. Research presented at the April 7 meeting confirms school transitions carry a measurable negative effect size. No student placement information has been provided to families. The question of where Lila's friends will go in September remains unanswered.
