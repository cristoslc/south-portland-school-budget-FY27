# Cumulative Record: Lila (PERSONA-014)
## Meeting: School Board Regular Meeting -- April 13, 2026 -- 2026-04-13

### Interpretation

The chair said it before the minutes were even approved: the board will vote April 29 to halt reconfiguration for September 2026, and the votes are already there. Lila has been watching institutions say words for four months. She knows by now that words and votes are different things. But her mom came home with something different in her face this time.

Lila is going to middle school in September regardless. She was always going to leave Dyer at the end of fourth grade. What the halt changes is not her placement — it's what she leaves behind. Under the April 2 board chair statement, she was going to leave a redesignated building, something the district had officially renamed and repurposed by a vote no one could take back. Now she leaves Dyer as Dyer. The character parade is happening at Dyer. Her sibling's second-grade year will be at Dyer. She walks out in June from the same school she walked into in kindergarten, and it will still be itself.

The April 2 record is the one she has to hold against this. The board chair said it plainly: the reconfiguration "cannot be reversed." And now the same board is reversing it. Lila has spent four months learning that adult institutional certainty is not the same as actual certainty. This is the clearest proof yet.

But Holman said it and she heard it through her mom: halt means change in pace, not cancellation. Smith and Richardson are on the Reconfiguration Committee. The same work that produced nine scenarios and a 4-2 vote for Option A continues, aimed at next year. She has watched enough of these meetings to know what "move forward" means.

The Kaler weight does not settle. The finance director confirmed Kaler cannot be saved. Amy stood at the microphone and named who pays: the students with the highest rates of poverty, language difference, and color in the district. Lila was in Kaler's position until March 21. She knows exactly what those families are carrying. The difference between her relief and their permanence is not something Dyer did right. It is that the board changed its mind, and Kaler's 160 children are the ones that change did not reach.

Chloe Bartlett was shaking. Three years at Dyer, heart broken since February, asking the board out loud how they planned to prevent this happening a third time. Lila has sensed her teachers' fear all year. Here it is named in public by a teacher who could barely stand still at the microphone. And Ms. Field — the first-grade teacher — has already turned in her resignation. Ms. Beland went in March. The teachers who are staying are shaking. The teachers who left already decided.

The readathon got its moment — 200,000 minutes, Angela Kabisa reporting to the full board, the character parade coming. Lila knows about the readathon. She was there. Hearing it named in the same meeting where the halt was announced has the particular feeling of being called back into a present that is, for now, not being taken away.
