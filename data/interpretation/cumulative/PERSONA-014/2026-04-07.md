# Cumulative Record: Lila (PERSONA-014)
## Meeting: School Board Regular Meeting -- April 7, 2026 -- 2026-04-07

### Interpretation

The budget passed four to two. Her mom came home quiet again.

But the vote is not what Lila keeps turning over. It's that she found out her school has not been safe this year — not just uncertain, not just threatened, but actually short-staffed and carrying safety concerns while she walked past that door every day without knowing. Andrew Gigler works in the functional life skills classroom she passes on the way to music. He told the board his room has been short one or two people nearly the entire year, with many days when no substitute came. Lila has spent four months afraid of what might happen to Dyer from the outside — budget votes, nine scenarios, slides with "CLOSED" on them — and now she learns that something was already wrong inside the building, all year.

Ms. Lefever drove back from Westbrook to say the same thing. She had started watching from home, in a different city, and felt compelled to return and stand at the microphone and say that without the OT positions, her classroom would not be okay. The adults who work in Lila's school are driving from other cities to fight for it. A Dyer parent filed formal paperwork with the city clerk — a recall affidavit against Board Chair DeAngelis, with "closure of one of our community schools" named as one of the reasons. Lila has watched four months of testimony, surveys, and 85% opposition documented in official minutes. Now the opposition has a different shape: legal steps. Adults fight harder when other tools have run out.

A teacher named Lynn McEwen said something that went straight through all the noise. Research shows school transitions carry a "negative effect size" — a measurement, a real number — meaning moving from one school to another actually sets children back. Not just feels bad. Sets them back. The board chair said in February that kids are flexible. Board member Richardson said in March that resilience is real. What neither of them mentioned is that the research says the opposite: transitions cost something measurable, and the cost lands on the children making them. Lila's fear was never parental anxiety rubbing off on her. It was right, and now there is a name for it.

And still: the superintendent confirmed that attendance zone details "are a part of the configuration planning" that the district "does not have at this point." The vote passed. The paperwork is filed. The budget moves to city council. The answer to where Maya and Sofia are going is still: we'll tell you eventually.

School starts in August.
