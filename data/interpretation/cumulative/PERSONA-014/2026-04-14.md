# Cumulative Record: Lila (PERSONA-014)
## Meeting: City Council Regular Meeting -- April 14, 2026 -- 2026-04-14

### Interpretation

The school budget hearing finally happened — and it happened one week late, because even the adults running the process weren't ready when they said they would be. Lila has been watching this all winter: the January 22 forum postponed to February 4, the March 30 budget vote failing and spilling into April 2, and now the April 7 hearing pushed to April 14 because the school board hadn't finished adopting the budget in time. The institutions that hold her future keep missing their own deadlines. She is nine years old and she has kept track of every one.

The thing her parents brought home from this meeting — the piece she has been turning over since morning — is the rule her dad tried to explain. The city council cannot say which school closes. State law, city charter: they can only set a dollar amount, and then the school board decides what to do with it. Lila has spent four months following the chain of responsibility, trying to find the person whose job it is to protect Dyer. The school board says the city council controls the money. The city council says the school board controls the buildings. She has been watching them point at each other since the February 10 joint workshop, when the board chair stood up and told the council to give clear guidance and they offered 3% to 6% instead. Now she has a name for why the pointing never stops: there is a rule that splits the decision in half, and no one person holds all of it.

She already knew about the 42 teachers. That number has been in her chest since December. But hearing it again — in this room, at this meeting, after the board voted 4-2 and the halt announcement and all of it — means it is still not settled. The teachers who got pink slips in March are still in pink-slip territory. Two positions were added back at April 7, but 42 is still the number circling her.

Most of the meeting was about the fire department and the library and parks. Her dad mentioned a dog park. She has been asking the same question since December — what is going to happen to my school — and the adults spent last night on a dog park. The June 9 vote is the last answer. It is also the last day of school.
