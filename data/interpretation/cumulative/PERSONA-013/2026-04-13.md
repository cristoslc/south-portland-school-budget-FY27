# Cumulative Record: Amira (PERSONA-013)
## Meeting: School Board Regular Meeting -- April 13, 2026 -- 2026-04-13

### Interpretation

The meeting opened with Angela — the same Angela who wrote that letter about music and belonging that someone read aloud at April 2 — giving the student representative report. She talked about field trips and talent shows and the high school musical. All the good things. I kept waiting for her to say something about the cuts, about what it actually feels like to be a student right now. She didn't. I'm not mad at her. She might not have had a choice about what she was supposed to say. But I keep sitting with it: the one formal student voice in the room, and it was only allowed to talk about the things that weren't broken.

Then a mom named Amy stood at the microphone and said what I've been thinking since March 23 but didn't know how to put into words. She had numbers. Kayler has the highest share of families who rent, the highest free and reduced lunch, the second-highest multilingual learners, the second-highest kids of color in the entire district. And Kayler is still closing while other schools get more time. She said it feels "profoundly unfair." I know that feeling. I've had it since March, watching the most vulnerable kids absorb the worst decisions.

A third-grade teacher named Chloe Bartlett stood at the microphone shaking. She asked the board: are we going to do this again next year? Are teachers going to be scared again next March? The board didn't answer. And then — after all of that — after the parents begged them to bring teachers back — the board voted three to two to put any additional money into savings. DeAngelis, Smith, Risch voted for reserves. Richardson and Holman voted no.

DeAngelis. The woman who told the story about students burning down a school because no one listened. The woman who said kids are "more flexible than parents." The woman who voted yes on the budget that cut percussion and computer science and PE. Now voting to put money in savings instead of recalling teachers. I'm not saying she doesn't care — I genuinely don't know what she's weighing. But the April she is voting in and the December she was speaking in are so far apart that I can barely hold them as the same person.

The chair also said reconfiguration is being paused until fall 2027 — which sounds like good news until you remember that Kayler is still closing this year. The kids whose school had the highest proportion of students who look like me, who eat free lunch like some of my friends, who are still learning English like some of my cousins — those kids don't get the pause. They get the closure. On time, as scheduled.
