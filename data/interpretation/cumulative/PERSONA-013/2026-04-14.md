# Cumulative Record: Amira (PERSONA-013)
## Meeting: City Council Regular Meeting -- April 14, 2026 -- 2026-04-14

### Interpretation

The meeting the school board couldn't make the deadline for. I keep coming back to that. The public hearing was supposed to happen on April 7 — the same night the board voted 4–2 to pass the budget with percussion and computer science and PE still cut. And they weren't ready. They had to push the whole thing back a week. I don't know what that means exactly. But all year the adults have been telling each other — and telling us, kind of — that this process has a timeline and they're following it. And then they missed their own deadline.

The most important thing I learned tonight is something I should have known months ago: the city council cannot save my band program. By law. By the City Charter. They can vote to raise or lower the total number. They cannot say "keep the percussion ed tech" or "restore the STEM teacher" or "don't close Kayler." That's the school board's call, not theirs.

I've been watching city council meetings since December. I watched councilors argue about school spending in February, saw Matthews say the people of South Portland can't afford any of the options, watched Scott make the connection between 80 families and 80 missing students. I thought somewhere in all of that there was a lever they could pull for specific things. There isn't. The five months of testimony at school board — 32 emails about the percussion ed tech, Matthew Emory at the microphone, Richardson saying "the joy and the light" before voting no — all of that happened in the only venue where specific decisions could actually be made. The public hearing at city council on April 14 was about the number. Just the number. Not about band. Not about Kayler's kids. Not about the ESOL teacher at Memorial. The number.

And voters decide on June 9. Not students. Not Cass, who said it wasn't fair. Not the seventh grader at Memorial who has never been to STEM or art class. Not me. The people deciding what my school looks like next year don't attend it.

Last night I was still deciding whether to walk up to a microphone. Tonight I learned that even city council, the body above the school board, has no authority over which specific things get cut. The door stayed open. The ceiling just got lower.
