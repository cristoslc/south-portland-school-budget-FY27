# Cumulative Record: Priya (PERSONA-005)
## Meeting: School Board Regular Meeting -- April 13, 2026 -- 2026-04-13

### Interpretation

Amy's testimony is the center of this meeting, and I need to stay with it before I move to anything else. She named the data — Kaylor has the highest renter rate, the highest free lunch eligibility, the second-highest multilingual learners, the second-highest children of color of any elementary school in this district — and then she named the structural asymmetry those numbers produce: while the rest of the district exhales because the board is slowing reconfiguration down, Kaylor families do not get that breath. The Finance Director confirmed what the budget slides were already suggesting: Kaylor's closure is not financially reversible. The pause is real. The closure is also real. And the community absorbing the irrevocable harm is the one with the highest concentration of renters, the most students qualifying for free lunch, the most multilingual learners.

Then the board chair called the next speaker's framing "elegant." That speaker had said we should stop being Kaylor parents or Brown parents and start being South Portland parents. I understand the solidarity impulse. But hearing that praised immediately after Amy had just laid out precisely which community is being asked to absorb the most concentrated and least reversible harm — that is the equity-erasure pattern I have been cataloguing since December, except this time the board chair is the one endorsing it. It is not the same as failing to hear an equity argument. It is actively rewarding the rhetorical move that dissolves it.

The special education data in this meeting followed the same logic as the achievement gap data in March: shown, then not used. Restraint and seclusion as a single aggregate bar. OT ratios presented districtwide without any school-level breakdown or demographic context. A parent asks where comprehensive incident data lives — the answer is a state dashboard the district does not proactively publish. Per-pupil spending at Small roughly $10,000 higher than at Skillin, flagged by a community member from the Finance Director's own slide, and the district's response is that the numbers are too complex to use without offering an alternative analysis. These are not data gaps. They are choices about what gets made visible and what gets left to the people doing their own homework.

The 3-2 contingency vote is the March 30 pattern at a smaller scale: the board had a choice between routing potential savings toward position restoration and routing them to a general buffer. Richardson and Holman voted to restore positions. They lost. The pattern has now repeated across every pool of discretionary money this budget season has surfaced.

I am pulling the May 2024 board minutes this week, because Action 20A and Action 20B were supposed to be the foundation that any reconfiguration rested on. They are not here. The reconfiguration is proceeding anyway. That sequence problem needs to be in the public record before anyone describes this process as equity-centered.
