# Cumulative Record: Priya (PERSONA-005)
## Meeting: School Board Regular Meeting -- April 7, 2026 -- 2026-04-07

### Interpretation

The April 7 meeting is where the $300,000 lost its promise. I carried that number out of April 2 with something close to hope — real money, union advocacy that worked, Richardson saying the right thing about where it should go. April 7 is the meeting where the administration's proposal routes it into contingency and one-year positions. Feller says plainly that it "doesn't make a lot of sense" to park dollars tied to unhoused students in a general buffer. Dr. Prince's answer amounts to: if there are no restrictions, we use it where we need it. That is the pattern I have been documenting since December — equity language in the funding description, equity language absent in the allocation. The $300,000 looked like leverage until I saw where the lever was pointed.

The functional life skills testimony is what I keep returning to. An ed tech came in to describe his classroom short-staffed for nearly the full year. The FLS teacher at Dyer drove from Westbrook to say on record that she has taken a real lunch break twice in fourteen years because she cannot leave those students unsupervised for safety reasons. The superintendent's response — safety concerns have not "risen" to his level — directly contradicts multiple frontline staff and the union president, who said roughly half of member concerns involve special education staffing and safety. The replacement plan is ed tech positions the district freely admits it cannot currently fill. These are students with the strongest legal protections and the highest support needs. What is happening in those classrooms is not a budget tradeoff. It is a service delivery failure in motion.

Then there is the behavioral strategist. Prior records tracked this as a position cut — alarming, but potentially restorable with the new state funds. April 7 reveals that the district is not recalling the incumbent but posting a credential-redesigned role that excludes a social worker with documented community impact. That is not a funding gap. That is a structural redesign that forecloses the specific person. Richardson names it directly: mental health priority for this district is declining. Two other social workers are also RIF'd. The $300,000 that might have changed this is going to contingency.

Jamie Watson's bus stop testimony made the transportation equity concern concrete and specific — more stops in the higher-income west side, fewer in Red Bank, advocacy that "hasn't happened this year." Holman made the connection in about ten seconds: the parents who cannot vote on a referendum tend to live where the bus stops don't go. Both facts deserve a policy response. The budget that passed 4-2 contains none. Neither does it contain any mechanism for Action 20A or 20B — the Board's own 2024 equity mandates, now two years undelivered.
