# Cumulative Record: Priya (PERSONA-005)
## Meeting: City Council Regular Meeting -- April 14, 2026 -- 2026-04-14

### Interpretation

The two tracks I have been running in parallel since December finally converged on April 14. For four months I watched the school board make structural decisions about the district's most diverse student populations without equity analysis, and I watched the city council approve TIFs, reject eviction protections, and route community funds through a priority ordering that consistently ranked everything above direct support for vulnerable residents. April 14 is the meeting where the school budget formally comes to the council, and the answer to how that convergence goes is: the same way everything else has gone.

The sixteen ed tech positions are my anchoring number from this entire budget season. I have been carrying them since January 15, when I first understood that they represent the frontline support infrastructure for students with IEPs and English learners — not overhead, not administration, but the people making inclusion mean something beyond a policy document. Now those positions are formally before the city council, and the presentation still does not tell anyone which schools lose which positions, which student populations bear the concentrated impact, or whether the schools with the highest concentrations of multilingual learners and students with disabilities are absorbing a disproportionate share. The data gap I have been documenting at the board level has now traveled to the council level intact.

The city manager's position paper language on building closures is the other thing I keep returning to. The explicit acknowledgment that "Council cannot dictate whether or not a school building is closed" is not a surprise — I know the governance structure — but its presence in a formal agenda document signals that closure is live and that the council is positioning itself at arm's length from that decision. I have watched these two bodies deflect accountability to each other all season: the board says it needs council guidance, the council says the board has operational authority. The April 14 framing continues that pattern, except now a school with the district's highest concentrations of renters, free lunch eligibility, and multilingual learners has a closure locked in, and neither body is holding the equity analysis.

What is genuinely new here is the process structure: the May 5 public hearing is a real advocacy window. The council cannot direct which positions are cut, but it can move the bottom line, which would require the board to reconvene and reallocate. That mechanism is still open. I have until May 5 to build the public record that forces the equity disaggregation question into council deliberation before the June 9 referendum. The Board's own 2024 equity mandates — Action 20A and 20B, still undelivered — belong in that testimony alongside the sixteen positions.
