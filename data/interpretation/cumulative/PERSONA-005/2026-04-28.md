# Cumulative Record: Priya (PERSONA-005)
## Meeting: 2026-04-28-city-council -- 2026-04-28

### Interpretation

At April 14, I documented the moment the equity disaggregation failure transferred from the school board to the city council — the same undisaggregated framing, the same 16 ed tech positions with no disability or EL context, arriving at the second governing body intact. April 28 is that finding, confirmed and deepened.

The school budget sits between Water Resource Protection and Finance on the workshop agenda. Five to fifteen minutes, like a plumbing contract. That structural framing is not an accident or an oversight — it is the most honest expression of how this council has processed the school budget all season. I have been documenting the two-body deflection since February 10, when the board called the council's refusal to set tax guidance "not sufficient, not adequate, not appropriate, not acceptable," and the council called the budget the board's operational domain. The April 28 agenda makes that dynamic visible in physical form: the schools are one line item among departments, and the question of which children absorb which cuts is not in the room.

The city manager's recommendation against fund balance forgiveness is the new piece I am carrying out of this meeting. The school believes the actual overage is at the lower end of the $1–$1.5 million range. The city manager's objection is capital reserves. I understand the fiscal argument. What I cannot accept is that argument being made without any analysis of what that overage — and the pressure to fill it through additional cuts — means for the 16 ed tech positions or the 42 teacher positions that still carry no school-level breakdown. The state funding gap is the context that recommendation cannot escape: 20% actual coverage against a 55% statutory obligation is not a school department management failure. It is the state systematically underfunding public education and letting the local property tax absorb what Augusta should be paying. The city manager protecting capital reserves against that backdrop is a choice, not a necessity, and it deserves to be named as such at the May 5 hearing.

The enrollment decline framing continues to treat 1,401 students shrinking to 1,080 as a staffing mismatch without asking who is still in those schools. I have been waiting since December for anyone in either governing body to state the obvious: the families with the easiest exit options are not the families with children in self-contained special education rooms or in EL services. The students who remained are, in aggregate, more likely to need the positions being cut. The district's own steering committee documented this. It has not appeared in a single budget presentation at the council level.

May 5 is what I have left before the June 9 referendum. I am writing testimony tonight.
