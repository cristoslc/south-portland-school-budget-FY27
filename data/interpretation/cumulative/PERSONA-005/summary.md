# Cumulative Summary: Priya (PERSONA-005)

### Current Understanding

As of April 14, 2026, Priya understands South Portland's school budget crisis as a system-wide failure with interlocking causes: a structural state funding gap (approximately 20% actual coverage against a 55% statutory obligation), years of management instability documented in a repeat "material weakness" audit finding, and serial mismanagement of grants designated for English learners and students with disabilities. The district faces an $8.4 million deficit and has proposed eliminating 78 positions, including 16 education technicians and 42 teachers. Neither the school board nor the city council has received a breakdown of which schools or student populations absorb the concentrated impact of those cuts.

The board voted 4-2 in March 2026 to adopt Option A reconfiguration, including the closure of Kayler Elementary — the district's highest-renter-rate, highest-free-lunch-eligibility, second-highest-multilingual-learner school. The Finance Director confirmed Kayler's closure is financially irreversible. The district's DEI Director role has been demoted to an instructional strategist in the teacher unit, with zero local FY27 funding and continuity dependent on a grant without a stated renewal commitment, eliminating the district's only BIPOC central office leader. The behavioral strategist position — covering nearly 60 students across four elementary schools — has been replaced through a credential redesign that excludes the current incumbent. Approximately $300,000 in state funds targeted to low-income and unhoused students was routed to contingency.

At the city level, an eviction moratorium failed 4-2, the state confirmed that fear-based income loss does not qualify for General Assistance, and $100,000 was appropriated through Project Home against a documented need of $168,000. The school budget has now reached the city council without disaggregated equity data. A May 5 public hearing and June 9 voter referendum are the remaining accountability windows. The Board's own 2024 equity mandates — Action 20A and Action 20B — remain undelivered.
