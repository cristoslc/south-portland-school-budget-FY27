# Cumulative Record: Maria (PERSONA-001)
## Meeting: City Council Regular Meeting -- April 21, 2026 -- 2026-04-21

### Interpretation

One week after April 14 clarified that the council can only vote on a total dollar figure — not on which teachers stay or which ed techs go — Maria sits through nearly five hours of city council on April 21 and learns almost nothing new about the budget that will shape her daughter's September. The two school-related items pass on the consent calendar without discussion. No floor time. No questions. Just a clerk reading order numbers and the council voting yes.

The Dowling vacancy is the detail that lodges. District 5's seat on the school board — empty, placed on the November ballot rather than filled by appointment. Maria knows what the school board calendar looks like between now and November: the April 29 reconfiguration vote, the May 5 council budget vote, the June 9 referendum. The board is making structural decisions right now with a gap at the table, and no one in that chamber tonight seemed to find it worth a single comment.

The Chamber of Commerce comment is harder to sit with. Thomas O'Boyle cited teacher layoffs and facility cuts as his argument for approving a developer-friendly land use plan — the school budget crisis as a rhetorical tool for a real estate argument. Maria has been watching this pattern since January 13, when the Mahoney project's potential school benefits were framed in terms of tax revenue that wouldn't materialize for decades. Now the Chamber is running the same logic in reverse: approve development today because it might fund schools someday. The timeline problem is the same. The pink slips went out March 18.

Councilor Scott's comment about housing building back school populations is the version of this argument Maria finds genuinely worth holding. Scott made the enrollment-displacement connection on March 10 in a way Maria recognized as true. She believes the logic. She does not know how long it takes. That gap — between a rezoned waterfront parcel and a child enrolled at Dyer or Brown — is the number nobody has given her.

The 21-speaker public comment that did not change the comprehensive plan outcome is one more data point in a pattern Maria has been building since the January forums. She is not surprised. She is adjusting. April 29 is eight days away. The school board is the room that matters.
