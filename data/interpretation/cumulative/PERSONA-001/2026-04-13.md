# Cumulative Record: Maria (PERSONA-001)
## Meeting: School Board Regular Meeting -- April 13, 2026 -- 2026-04-13

### Interpretation

Maria walks into this meeting twelve hours before the city council workshop she has spent weeks getting people ready for, and the first thing the chair says is that the board is moving to pause September reconfiguration. She has been awake over this for months. The group chat lights up before she can fully process what she actually heard.

She is relieved. She is also careful about what the relief means. The Reconfiguration Committee continues with two board members. DeAngelis says "slowing down," not "stopping." The structural problems that produced nine options and 72 pink slips are all still there. Maria has been in too many rooms this year where an announcement felt like a breakthrough and turned out to be a longer road. She is filing this under real but partial. The April 29 vote will tell her more.

What she does not file under partial is Beth Kellogg's resignation. Brown's principal. Effective June 30 — right when every staffing transition in the district begins. No search timeline, no interim arrangement, nothing. Just a name read off a list and the meeting moves on. Maria knows how much a principal matters in an elementary building. She has been tracking the support staff erosion since December 17, when she first saw sixteen ed techs on a cut list, and leadership stability is the layer underneath all of it that she had not thought to add to her inventory. She is adding it now.

The OT ratio announcement is the moment the evening tips into something harder. Director Cox tells the board the new districtwide ratio will be one occupational therapist for every thirty-six students — and then says she would not advocate for those numbers. That is a district official, on the record, describing a caseload she herself thinks is too high. Richardson raises that both data years in the restraint and seclusion chart used the embedded model, meaning they are implementing a change whose impact they cannot predict. Cox acknowledges this directly. The board moves forward anyway.

The $360,000 contingency vote is where the pattern Maria has been tracking since December sharpens into something she can name precisely. Three board members had the chance to use available savings to restore positions and voted to save the money instead. Richardson and Holman wanted the teachers back. The majority said no. Maria understands the fiscal logic — the FY26 deficit might hit $1.1 million, the fund balance is gone — but seventy-two people are still holding pink slips, and tonight the board's revealed preference, when the choice was explicit and on the table, was the savings account.

Then Ketchen mentions FY28 will need "careful planning" to stay under 6%, and Maria drives home doing the same math she has been doing since January.
