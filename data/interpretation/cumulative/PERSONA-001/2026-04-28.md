# Cumulative Record: Maria (PERSONA-001)
## Meeting: City Council Regular Meeting -- April 28, 2026 -- 2026-04-28

### Interpretation

Maria learned at April 14 that the council cannot protect specific teachers or programs — they can only vote on a total dollar figure. She recalibrated her aim that night and pointed herself at the school board. April 28 confirms the recalibration was right, but it also adds a new layer of frustration she did not fully anticipate: the council is not even treating the school budget as a primary agenda item. Item nine of thirteen. She sat through Water Resource Protection, Code Enforcement, Planning, and Economic Development before anyone said a word about the 42 teachers.

She has been doing this since December. She knows the pattern. She knew, walking in, that tonight would not be the meeting where anything was decided — May 12 is the parking lot vote, that is where the math gets finalized. What she did not fully know until tonight is that the fund balance fight is closer to resolution than it appeared. A majority of councilors signaled at April 14 that they support forgiving some of the school's fund balance debt. The school is saying it needs $1 to $1.5 million forgiven, not the $3 million figure the city's conservative accounting produced. The city manager's position paper recommends against forgiveness, citing capital project impacts — but the city manager's recommendation and what this council actually does have diverged before. Maria has watched this council absorb staff recommendations and then move in a different direction often enough that she does not read "staff recommends no" as the final word. The majority signal is real, and she is holding it as a real thing, carefully.

What she cannot hold carefully anymore is the absence of specificity. The 42-teacher number has been in every document since February 4. She has heard it at forums, at workshops, at council chambers, in the city manager's position paper. What she has never heard — not once, not from the board, not from the district, not from this council — is which teachers, at which schools, losing which programs. The parking lot goes to May 12. She is going. She is bringing that question with her, again.
