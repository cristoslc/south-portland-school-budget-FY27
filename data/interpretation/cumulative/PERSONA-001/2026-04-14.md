# Cumulative Record: Maria (PERSONA-001)
## Meeting: City Council Regular Meeting -- April 14, 2026 -- 2026-04-14

### Interpretation

Maria has been pointing toward April 14 since Scott announced the council workshop calendar on March 19. She put the date in every parent channel she runs and told people: this is the one. She went to the April 7 city council meeting and found the school budget hearing had been pushed — board hadn't transmitted the final numbers in time. Tonight she gets the presentation she waited two weeks for. And it tells her almost nothing she doesn't already know.

Forty-two teachers. Sixteen ed techs. Enrollment down 23 percent in four years. State covering 20 percent of costs when its own formula says 55. Fund balance gone. She has been carrying all of this since December. Hearing it restated at a council dais doesn't change any of it. What does change tonight — what lands as something genuinely new — is the city manager's position paper laying out the legal constraint plainly: the council cannot direct the district to keep or cut any specific position, program, or building. Under state law and the city charter, they vote on the total dollar amount, and the school board decides what fits inside it.

Maria sits with that for a long moment. She has spent five months watching this council absorb information about behavioral strategists and immigrant children and empty desks and a superintendent search designed to minimize community voices. She tracked their guidance, their votes, their competing capital ambitions. She thought, at least implicitly, that if enough parents showed up and enough councilors felt it, there was some mechanism by which they could intervene. Tonight the city manager's paper names the mechanism precisely: there isn't one. The council sets the size of the envelope. The school board decides what goes inside. The decisions Maria has been trying to influence were never in this building to begin with.

Then the council rolled through ten more departments — police, fire, the library Kevin Davis begged for in March before it was stripped from Mahoney, parks and recreation, public works. Maria sat through all of it. The school budget was the first item on a long agenda and it did not come back.

She has the school board listening sessions in her calendar. The April 29 reconfiguration vote. The May 5 council budget vote. She is not done. She is adjusting her aim.
