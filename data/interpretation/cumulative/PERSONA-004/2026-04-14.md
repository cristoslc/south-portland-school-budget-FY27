# Cumulative Record: Marcus (PERSONA-004)
## Meeting: City Council Regular Meeting -- April 14, 2026 -- 2026-04-14

### Interpretation

The fight has moved across town, and I'm sitting here realizing the council can't actually do what I needed them to do. All these months — December inauguration speeches, junkyard licenses, a four-hour building workshop, the joint session that evaporated a week later — I've been watching the council and telling myself that eventually, in this room, someone would stand up and say "restore the teaching positions." Tonight confirms that's not how it works. The city manager's position paper makes it plain: the council moves a bottom line. It doesn't direct cuts or restorations. Every specific fight I've been tracking since March 2 — the behavior strategist, the percussion ed tech, the science positions, Feller's conditions — none of that is the council's domain. The board already passed it four to two on April 7 and handed the council a number. The council's only move is to fund it higher or lower.

What I keep returning to is the enrollment framing. The fiscal context cites a 23% elementary decline — 1,401 to 1,080 students over four years. Elementary. The high school numbers are not disaggregated. The 42 teacher eliminations are not broken out by building level. So the narrative that's going to drive this referendum — staffing grew while enrollment dropped — is built on elementary enrollment data being used to justify district-wide cuts that reach into SPHS. That's not analysis. That's a rhetorical frame with a selective denominator, and it's going to be everywhere on social media between now and June 9.

I've known since January 15 that the 82-positions-and-300-students framing was embedded in the council's planning materials. I've watched it migrate from school board presentations to council floor rhetoric to public comment. Tonight it's in the document that opens the budget workshop. The counter-narrative — those positions were hired for asylum-seeking families, special education complexity, behavioral health needs the district itself approved — has never gotten equal footing in a formal presentation. That is the six-week campaign ahead of us. Not the council chamber. The community.

The June 9 referendum is where this ends or continues. The council votes May 5. The public votes June 9. A failed referendum likely means deeper cuts than what's already been done. I need to be at SPEA's table tomorrow.
