# Cumulative Record: Marcus (PERSONA-004)
## Meeting: 2026-04-28-city-council -- 2026-04-28

### Interpretation

The second council budget workshop ended the same way the first one did: nothing decided, everything deferred, and the specific dollar math Marcus needs to understand still not publicly calculated.

What's new is the fund balance forgiveness question. I knew the fund balance was depleted — that's been on the record since March 9. What I didn't know until this week is that the mechanism being discussed at the council level is debt relief, not a straight appropriation increase. The school owes the city something in the range of $1 to $1.5 million for the fund balance overage — less than half the $3 million the city had been holding in reserve as a conservative estimate. That's the school being more careful about its own ask than the city anticipated. It's a real opening, if the council takes it.

But the City Manager is recommending zero forgiveness. His staff's position is that forgiving the debt either pulls money from city capital needs or forces a city-side tax increase to compensate. And apparently a majority of councilors at April 14 said they're open to some forgiveness. So there's an elected official majority pulling one direction and professional staff pulling the other. I've watched this dynamic at the school board since December — Dr. Prince says what he recommends, the board mostly follows. I don't know if the same pattern holds here. I need to know before May 12.

What I cannot find anywhere is the dollar-to-positions conversion. If the city forgives $1 million, how many of the 32 recalled colleagues come back? If they forgive $1.5 million, what does that buy? That number should be on a slide. The Finance Director can calculate it. But it wasn't presented tonight, which means we're heading into the final workshop without the one piece of information that connects a political negotiation about debt relief to the classroom reality of what actually happens in September.

The framing is still there. Eighty-two positions, 300 students. The state covering 20% of what it owes — the actual driver of this crisis — stayed in the background again. The counter-narrative has not made it into an official document, and we are six weeks from June 9.
