# Cumulative Record: Marcus (PERSONA-004)
## Meeting: School Board Regular Meeting -- April 13, 2026 -- 2026-04-13

### Interpretation

The reconfiguration pause gave the room a moment of relief, and I understand why — parents have been fighting since February and they needed something. But I couldn't let myself settle into it, because the pause and the 40 positions are separate tracks. Abigail Ketchen said it herself: closing Kaylor accounts for roughly half the total cuts. The other half — the teachers, the ed techs, the programs — those happen whether any school closes or not. The chair announced a delay in the reconfiguration timeline and nobody mentioned that 32 colleagues are still on a recall list waiting to find out if their careers in this district are over.

Three more resignations. Two classroom teachers, one principal. The classroom teachers go to the recall list — which means the list grows while the positions it was supposed to fill aren't being posted. I've been watching this since January 12, when non-backfilling of retirements first appeared on an agenda slide, and the pattern is the same: the district loses people it didn't budget to lose, and the attrition count and the RIF count compound each other silently.

Sarah Gay brought 187 respondents — two-thirds of the bargaining unit — and put 85.6% on the record saying the district has not been transparent with us about how these cuts were communicated. That is a documented result from the people doing the work, not a handful of voices at a microphone. The board heard it and moved on to the next item.

The vote that clarifies everything is the 3-2 on contingency. Richardson and Holman said bring back positions — Holman specifically named related arts at the middle school. Smith, Risch, and DeAngelis said put the $360,000 into reserve. I understand the fund balance is depleted. I've known that since March 9. But when the board's first instinct with new money is institutional financial repair rather than restoring what was cut from classrooms, that is information. It tells me where their priorities sit when they have to choose.

Chloe Bartlett asked whether we're going to go through another year of fear. Ketchen's FY28 modeling — already a touch over 6% under current assumptions, with the new SPTA contract as a major unknown — is the closest thing to an answer anyone gave her. Nobody said it out loud to her face. The structural trap March 23 confirmed is extending forward, and a third-year teacher shaking at the microphone didn't get a direct response.

### Emotional Register

Marcus carries April 13 as the meeting where the board was handed a small amount of new money and chose, three to two, not to bring anyone back — and then adjourned while Chloe Bartlett's unanswered question about next March hung in the air alongside a Finance Director's confirmation that FY28 is already trending over the same ceiling that produced this year's bloodbath. He is not surprised, and the absence of surprise is its own kind of grief.
