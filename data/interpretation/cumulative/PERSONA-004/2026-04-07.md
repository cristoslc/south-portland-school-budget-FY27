# Cumulative Record: Marcus (PERSONA-004)
## Meeting: School Board Regular Meeting -- April 7, 2026 -- 2026-04-07

### Interpretation

The April 7 public hearing — the date Marcus has been circling since the budget calendar locked in on March 19 — arrived and ended with the budget passing four to two. The board found $655,000 in health insurance savings last Friday and had six days to decide what to do with it. The administration came in with slides. The teachers' association had not seen those slides before tonight.

Dr. Prince said it himself when pressed: the district had not specifically gone back to the SPTA table about the position recommendations in front of the board. That pattern has run since December 10, when the board chose a superintendent search firm that recommended keeping community out of candidate screening, through March 30, when reconfiguration was adopted the morning before meet-and-consult was scheduled to begin. Tonight it happens with found money and a room full of teachers who spent the day not knowing if their position was the "related arts teacher" line on a new slide. Jamie Watson named it exactly: five colleagues, applying to other districts in the margins of a workday spent in uncertainty.

Two teachers are coming back. One year only. Elementary. Grade determined by enrollment. Marcus teaches at the high school — no positions at SPMS or SPHS were proposed for restoration. The $655,000 was divided: SPTA received the largest share, $238,000, allocated between two positions and $15,000 for voluntary summer work. Sarah Gay did the math at the microphone: $15,000 covers approximately one teacher working 277 hours, or five hours each for fifty teachers. Five hours to move a classroom, learn a new team, restructure curriculum. Feller called moving a classroom "like moving a house." He's right. The $15,000 doesn't begin to address what reconfiguration actually requires.

The behavior strategist piece has been on the record since March 23. Tonight's resolution: post a Board Certified Behavior Analyst rather than recall the incumbent. A different credential, a different title, a different person. Richardson pressed on why recall wasn't used and named what this means alongside the two other social worker cuts. Marcus doesn't know whether this is an honest administrative judgment about credential fit or a workaround — but he knows the community showed up to fight for specific work serving sixty specific children, and the district posted a different job.

The survey numbers — 92% negative morale, 84% saying administration should face deeper cuts first, 76% opposing the current budget — are now on the public record, delivered on the night this board voted four to two to send the budget to the city council. DeAngelis called it "not the final step." Technically true. But the leverage picture has changed. The fight that lived in that room for four months just moved across town.
