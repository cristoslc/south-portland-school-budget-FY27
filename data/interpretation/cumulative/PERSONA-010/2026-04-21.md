# Cumulative Record: Ben (PERSONA-010)
## Meeting: City Council Regular Meeting -- April 21, 2026 -- 2026-04-21

### Interpretation

The lede is not the 5-2 vote. Every outlet in town is going to write that the comprehensive plan passed, which it did. The lede is the two people who stood at that microphone, one after the other, and said they are in cancer treatment living 700 feet from the tanks. Edward Reiner, 25 Ballard Street — bladder cancer. Leslie Gatcombe-Hines, 47 Elmwood Avenue — genetic testing ruled out the hereditary causes, and she has spent 21 years in a neighborhood the EPA says carries a moderate cancer risk. These are not policy arguments. They are people describing what their doctors told them. I am calling both of them this week, and that is the piece.

Underneath those testimonies, Councilor West dropped fence line monitoring data into the record: fourteen readings over the state limit at the Sunoco site, one of them seven times the limit. That is not a community group's press release. That is a sitting council member citing data during a vote. Four of her colleagues then voted down an amendment that would have required the city to model emissions at full operational capacity before permitting residential development near those tanks. I am not suggesting the no votes were wrong — Councilor Scott made a fair point that modeling methodology language belongs in an ordinance, not a comprehensive plan — but the collision of those two facts in a single meeting is the gap I exist to fill.

The CALUPA 27 story is the one the vote does not tell. Planning Director Nevajda confirmed staff proposed it to thread the needle between developers and the shipyard preservation group. Councilor West got on a plane thinking it would pass and landed to find it failed, apparently by a thin margin in a committee with unfilled seats. Now a resident says he has met with the Packard family and is "optimistic" about green space — an aside at the end of a three-hour public comment session, not a verified negotiating update. None of this is resolved by the plan's passage. What it means, and what I need to explain to readers, is that the real decisions about what gets built near Bug Light will be made in a multi-year zoning process the council has not yet started, by a council that may have three different members after November.

The school board seat — Dowling's old District 5 vacancy — goes to the November ballot, not to a council appointment. The board will operate one seat short through the June 9 referendum. That is six people deciding the most consequential budget in a generation. I am not sure my readers know that.
