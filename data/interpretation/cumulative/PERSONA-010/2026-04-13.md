# Cumulative Record: Ben (PERSONA-010)
## Meeting: School Board Regular Meeting -- April 13, 2026 -- 2026-04-13

### Interpretation

Ben has been tracking equity as the season's through-line since March 2 — the Skillin defense, Tyler Smith's comment about busing Black and Brown kids, DeAngelis's "abhorrent" framing of west-side isolation, the DEI director demotion at negligible savings. April 13 inverts that story entirely, and the inversion comes from a parent at a public comment microphone, not from anyone on the board.

Chair DeAngelis opens by announcing the reconfiguration timeline is being paused — before a single agenda item — attributing it to two and a half hours of constituent calls and strong board support for delay. That's a real story: public pressure moved the board on its own calendar. But the pause does not touch Kaylor. And then Amy stands up and names what the board never named across four months of equity-framed deliberation: Kaylor has the highest share of renters, the highest free-and-reduced lunch rate, the second-highest concentrations of multilingual learners and students of color among all South Portland elementaries. Families at other schools get more time. Kaylor's 160 kids don't. Ben is careful not to characterize this as deliberate — he does not know that — but Amy's statement is now in the public record, unanswered by the board, and it sits directly alongside an equity framing the board itself constructed and deployed through the entire spring. That gap is not conspiracy. It is a question.

The per-school cost breakdown is the data Ben has wanted since December 17. Ketchem allocated roughly $5.7M–$7.8M per elementary, with Kaylor closure saving about $4M — half the total reduction package. He needs to call her on methodology before any figures go to print. A parent at public comment did his own per-pupil math and came up with figures the finance director neither confirmed nor disputed. That call is Thursday.

Ketchem's FY28 warning is the season's quiet kicker. She is already running roll-forward models landing "a touch over 6%" before attrition, with health insurance, EPS formula uncertainty, and an as-yet-uncalculated SPTA time-beyond-contract liability all unresolved. A third-year Dyer teacher named Chloe Bartlett asked shaking whether next March brings the same fear. Nobody answered her directly. That question is Ben's closing paragraph — because the structural math that produced FY27's crisis does not close with the FY27 budget.

The board's formal request that the city absorb $360K in SRO and snow removal costs turns Richardson's April 7 rhetorical question into actual policy. That's the city-school connective thread he has been pulling since December, now formalized with a vote and a dollar figure.
