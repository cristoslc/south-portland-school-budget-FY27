# Cumulative Record: Ben (PERSONA-010)
## Meeting: City Council Regular Meeting -- April 28, 2026 -- 2026-04-28

### Interpretation

Ben has been tracking the fund balance story since December 8 — zero cushion, depleted reserves, the quiet mechanics of how the city backstops a school deficit. April 28 is where that abstraction becomes a negotiating table with competing numbers. The city and the school disagree on what the school actually owes: city staff put the overage at roughly $3 million, school officials believe it's closer to $1 to $1.5 million — a $1.5 to $2 million spread between estimates. The agenda also reveals something Ben didn't fully register coming out of April 14: a council majority already indicated at that workshop they'd forgive some of the balance. The question tonight isn't whether to forgive. It's how much.

City finance staff is recommending zero forgiveness, laying out the downstream math explicitly: either capital purchases get delayed, or the city-side tax levy has to rise to replace the funds that would otherwise replenish. Meanwhile the school department says its FY27 budget work is on hold — they need a dollar figure from council before they can finalize their own numbers. That's the structural dependency Ben has been tracking across five months rendered as a specific institutional moment: two arms of the same city government, occupying the same workshop, unable to finish their respective books until one decides what the other owes.

The parking lot math lands an additional punch Ben hadn't calculated. If all outstanding items from workshops one and two are adopted, the city-side tax rate hits 6.5% — up from the 5.1% baseline — before the school budget referendum even lands. Councilor West submitted two items by proxy: removing $250,000 in fund balance used to buy down the rate, and directing surplus toward a capital building reserve. The combined household hit keeps accumulating in fragments nobody has assembled for the public in one sentence.

The two school department PDFs listed in the agenda attachments — their content not summarized anywhere in the position paper — are what Ben needs before he can file. The school budget page is referenced. The documents are public. But the gap between what the agenda tells a reporter and what was actually presented in the room is the bridge The Forecaster exists to build.
