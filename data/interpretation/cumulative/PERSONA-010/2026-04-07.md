# Cumulative Record: Ben (PERSONA-010)
## Meeting: School Board Regular Meeting -- April 7, 2026 -- 2026-04-07

### Interpretation

The budget passes. Four months, twenty-odd meetings, an $8 million gap, 78 positions — and on April 7 the board votes 4-2 and sends it to council. That's the news. But the meeting that produces the vote is also the meeting where the most damaging accountability information of the entire season comes not from the administration's presentation but from a parent at a public comment mic.

Ben has been tracking the district's pattern of selective transparency since March 2, when he noted the administration was open about numbers it wanted discussed and opaque about data it didn't. April 7 is the clearest example yet. The administration's presentation leads with the $655,000 health insurance windfall — genuinely good news, professionally delivered — and walks through a three-way framework of fund balance, reserves, and contingency that finally gives general readers a vocabulary for what's been broken. That's the version of this district Ben wishes he'd been covering all season. Then Meredith Diamond walks to the microphone and reads from the FY25 audit: $2.37 million overspent without authorization across two fiscal years, $400,000 in grants lost to a missed reimbursement window, $123,000 written back to the state. The audit was due to the board by December 30, 2025. None of it appeared in the administration's slides.

Ben has been assembling the structural accountability story since December 8. The revolving finance leadership. The repeat audit findings. The chronic under-budgeting documented by Ketchem herself on April 2. Diamond just handed him the specific document that ties it together, and she found it herself. He is calling the city clerk's office first thing.

The OT-to-ed-tech swap is the human story he needed alongside the accountability piece. A teacher from one of the affected classrooms says the replacement positions haven't been filled, that the weekly postings were quietly removed, and that she doesn't expect her colleagues back. The administration confirms the slots are unstaffed. Sarah Gay does the math at the mic that nobody in the room did: $15,000 for teacher summer work, at district wages, buys roughly five hours per person. That's what budget-neutral reconfiguration costs on the ground for the people being asked to move their classrooms. He can write that paragraph.

Richardson's floor statement is the institutional accountability line he has been building toward since December — she says out loud that the board handed the administration a number that was always going to cause harm, and the lost jobs are the proof. And then the room learns mid-meeting, from a parent, that the vice chair resigned an hour earlier. The board chair had said only he "is not able to be present tonight." The Portland Press Herald broke the news before the board did.
