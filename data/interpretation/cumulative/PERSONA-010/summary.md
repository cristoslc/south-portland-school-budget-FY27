# Cumulative Summary: Ben (PERSONA-010)

### Current Understanding

As of April 14, Ben has spent four months covering two parallel municipal fiscal crises that land on the same property tax bill. The school district's FY27 superintendent's budget passed 4-2 on April 7 and is now before the city council, proposing a 6% school tax increase — approximately $257 per year on the median $514,000 assessed home — with 78 positions eliminated and Kaylor Elementary closed. The structural roots of the crisis are documented: elementary enrollment declined 23% over four years while staffing grew by 82 positions; seven finance directors in six years prevented institutional knowledge from forming; no minimum fund balance policy existed; and predictable expenses were systematically under-budgeted for at least four consecutive fiscal years.

The June 9 referendum is 56 days away. Under state law and the City Charter, the council's authority is limited to the total school budget figure; all specific decisions on positions, programs, and school closures belong to the school board alone. The September 2026 reconfiguration timeline has been acknowledged as unworkable, with a formal pause vote expected April 29. No Title VI legal analysis for the Kaylor closure has been completed or placed in the public record. The FY25 audit — due to the board December 30, 2025 — contained findings that a community member surfaced at a public meeting; those findings did not appear in the administration's own presentation.

Three additional cost vectors compound the school tax increase: sewer rates rising approximately 22% per year through FY29, a November 2026 public safety bond (~$87M), and a potential November 2027 Mahoney bond (~$70–76M). No elected body has assembled these into a single household impact figure. Labor costs under existing contracts already grow faster than 6% per year, and the finance director's FY28 roll-forward model lands above 6% before attrition.
