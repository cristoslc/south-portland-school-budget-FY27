# Cumulative Summary: Ben (PERSONA-010)

### Current Understanding

Ben has covered twenty municipal meetings since December 1, 2025, and now understands the South Portland school budget crisis as a three-layer systemic failure, not a one-season budgeting error. The district's $8 million structural gap — requiring 78 position eliminations, a school closure, and an elementary reconfiguration — is the product of five years of revolving finance leadership (seven directors in six years), four consecutive years of chronic under-budgeting of predictable expenses by six figures annually, and a decade-long staffing increase of 82 positions during a 23% elementary enrollment decline. The fund balance is zero against the district's own 9–12% minimum reserve guideline; the audit reveals repeat material weaknesses with $2.37 million overspent across two fiscal years without authorization.

The board passed its FY27 budget 4-2 on April 7, closed Kaler Elementary 6-1 on March 30, and approved elementary reconfiguration 4-2 — but the reconfiguration timeline has been paused until at least April 29, with September 2026 implementation no longer viable. The board voted 4-1 to formally request the city absorb $360,000 in SRO and snow removal costs. Finance Director Ketchem's FY28 roll-forward model lands "a touch over 6%" before attrition, meaning the structural math that produced this crisis has not been resolved by the cuts. Vice Chair Dowling resigned, and the board operates at six members through the June 9 referendum.

Beyond schools, Ben now tracks four fiscal variables converging on the same property tax bill — school tax increase, 22% annual sewer rate hikes through FY29, an $87 million public safety bond targeting November 2026, and a $70–76 million Mahoney bond deferred to November 2027 — with no elected body having publicly stated the combined household impact against the $514,000 average assessed value. The immigration enforcement crisis collided directly with the budget process when families kept children home from school out of fear, the state blocked General Assistance eligibility for fear-based income loss, and the council voted 6-1 for $100,000 in Project Home rental assistance with Matthews citing school pink slips in her lone dissent. Two cancer patients living near petroleum tanks have brought the eastern waterfront development conflict into the human register, and the comprehensive plan passed 5-2 without requiring emissions modeling at full operational capacity before residential development near the tanks.
