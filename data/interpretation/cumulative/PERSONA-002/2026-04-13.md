# Cumulative Record: David (PERSONA-002)
## Meeting: School Board Regular Meeting -- April 13, 2026 -- 2026-04-13

### Interpretation

The location-based budget breakdown is the data correction I have been waiting for since February. The ~$400K figure for Kaler that circulated through reconfiguration debates since the March 2 workshop was always facilities only — central services, transportation, and instruction were never allocated against it. Ketchem's location-sorted analysis shows the full allocated cost of running Kaler is closer to $4 million. That number reframes the entire reconfiguration argument. Roughly half of all FY27 reductions this district fought through since December can be traced to a single building's full operating cost. The financial logic behind reconfiguration was always stronger than the circulating figures suggested — now the arithmetic is actually visible.

The FY26 deficit update moves in the opposite direction. A range of $80K to $1.1 million at three-quarters through the year is not a projection — it is an acknowledgment that the books are not settled enough to give a number. And then the assistant superintendent disclosed that SPTA time-beyond-contract hours have been untracked all year and not yet submitted for payment. Those costs will arrive on top of whatever deficit is already running. Combined with the unreconciled benefits and withholding accounts Ketchem confirmed are still under remediation — a problem dating back years, now being addressed — the FY26 close is going to produce at least one more uncomfortable disclosure. The district has not yet finished finding its own floor. That is not an accusation; it is the pattern the auditor documented in February and Ketchem has been correcting since March.

The unanimous vote to request a 1% tax increase specifically to seed contingency is the most functionally sound board action since setting the 6% ceiling in December. Not because $540K solves anything — the 9% benchmark Ketchem named two weeks ago implies a target of roughly $6.75M, and $540K is less than ten percent of that — but because the board unanimously agreed that reserve accumulation takes priority over position restoration. The motion to use 0.5% to restore positions failed for lack of a second. That outcome is right, and it is new: this board has not previously produced a formal vote placing institutional stability over political pressure.

The SRO and snow removal ask is analytically contested. One board member made the point I would have made: it is the same taxpayer. Whether the district saves $360K or the city saves $360K, the same property tax base absorbs it. The question is whether the city actually absorbs it or passes it through the mill rate — and whether the district follows through on putting it into contingency if they do.

Ketchem says a preliminary FY28 model exists and light attrition keeps it under 6%. She named six variables that could make that wrong. That is honest uncertainty. The FY28 structural math I have been tracking since March 23 is no longer unmodeled — it is modeled and fragile, which is better than unmodeled and ignored.
