# Cumulative Record: David (PERSONA-002)
## Meeting: 2026-04-28-city-council -- 2026-04-28

### Interpretation

The fund balance forgiveness debate is the moment this budget cycle reveals its final structural flaw: the council's own pre-signaled lean toward forgiveness converted tonight's workshop into a ratification ceremony dressed as a deliberation. I have been tracking this pattern since December 1 — governing bodies conducting proceedings whose outcomes are already determined — but this version has a specific edge to it. The school district knows the answer is yes. So instead of making a case for forgiveness, they tie the ask to a specific dollar commitment with formal budget implications. That is not a conversation about fiscal responsibility. That is a closing move.

The number gap matters and I am not letting it go. The city budgeted $3 million conservatively. The school says the actual figure is $1 to $1.5 million. A $1.5 million spread on the same question, negotiated without a shared model, is the same controls failure the February 3 auditor documented — different context, same root. The city manager at least named the real trade-off plainly: forgiveness either delays capital purchases or raises city-side taxes. That is the kind of institutional candor I have been crediting when I see it. The problem is the council appears to have already discounted it.

What I genuinely respect is Councilor West's written request to seed a capital building reserve from any surplus fund balance or school repayment above the 12% threshold. That is the discipline the district has needed since December — a rule that captures surplus before it becomes available for the next political ask. It is also consistent with West being the only person in this process who regularly does the math and then has to watch the vote go the other way.

The parking lot math puts us at roughly 6.5% on the tax rate if all of this goes through. We started this process with a council guidance band of 3 to 6 percent. The 6% ceiling the school board built to — and that I credited as a genuine achievement at the March 23 workshop — now sits inside a combined city-school package that exceeds it. Nobody has put a multi-year projection on the table. We are negotiating a one-time fix to a structural problem, and year three is still unmodeled.
