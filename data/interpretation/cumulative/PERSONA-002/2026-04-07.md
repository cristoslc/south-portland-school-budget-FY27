# Cumulative Record: David (PERSONA-002)
## Meeting: School Board Regular Meeting -- April 7, 2026 -- 2026-04-07

### Interpretation

The health insurance number is the clearest good news of this entire budget cycle. Final rate: 5.14%, versus the 11.5% ceiling the board has been modeling since March. That releases $655K, and the administration had an allocation proposal ready the same evening. That is responsive. And for the first time, someone explained the actual mechanics of how fund balance gets rebuilt: hold contingency, don't spend it, it sweeps to undesignated fund balance at year end. Simple. Correct. It should have been on a slide in December.

But April 7 is also the meeting where my mental model of the district's actual financial condition had to move downward — again. Finance Director Ketchem confirmed, almost in passing, that some reserve accounts have negative balances. Not zero. Negative. Funds were spent from those accounts without the governance oversight the district now describes as required. I have been using "fund balance: zero" since April 2. That framing was insufficient. The district's reserve position is below zero in at least some accounts, and the magnitude has not been quantified publicly.

Then a community member stood up and read from the FY25 independent audit. $2.37 million in unauthorized overspending. $400K in grant reimbursements left unclaimed. $600K in employee benefits under-withheld. The district disputed none of it. I went home and pulled the audit. The figures hold. This reframes four months of budget narrative. The structural gap did not accumulate solely from enrollment decline and contract under-budgeting — it accumulated against a backdrop of financial controls failures that were documented in official audit findings and apparently not elevated to the board's budget conversation until a parent forced it into the record.

The MaineCare billing comparison is the number I cannot let go. Portland bills $600K per year for comparable services. Rockland — a smaller district — bills $500K. South Portland bills $70K. The response was that it is "worth exploration." That is not an answer. It is a placeholder for an answer. If the district had been billing at Portland's rate for the past three years, the fund balance conversation looks different.

The board voted 4-2 to advance the FY27 budget to council. That was the necessary procedural outcome. It does not change the fact that the reconfiguration implementation costs — transportation routing, bathroom compliance, building readiness, IEP transitions — were not estimated before the vote. The $179K contingency that was cited is not a budget for a district-wide school reorganization affecting a thousand students. It is an educated guess that something will go wrong.
