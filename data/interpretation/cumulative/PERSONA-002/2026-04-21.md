# Cumulative Record: David (PERSONA-002)
## Meeting: 2026-04-21-city-council -- 2026-04-21

### Interpretation

The comprehensive plan passed five to two, and David finds himself unable to feel much about it. Not because the vote was wrong — the planning director's presentation was technically solid — but because four years of process produced a governing document with no fiscal quantification attached to its most consequential land-use decision. The Eastern Waterfront is the centerpiece of the city's long-term tax base argument, and the staff never put a number on what it actually yields. A public commenter with an economics background estimates $2.5 million net after expenses. Nobody challenged it. Nobody offered an alternative. That number — less than two percent of the city budget — is now the only figure in the record, unchecked, as the city heads toward a November public safety bond referendum and a school budget referendum on June 9.

What the planning director did quantify is the timeline, and it is longer than the political conversation has ever acknowledged. State certification, then roughly three years of zoning work, then development review, then construction lag. New Eastern Waterfront revenue is five to seven-plus years away at minimum. David has been watching the school board, the city council, and now a public referendum process all proceed as if the tax base is a near-term lever. Milan's presentation clarifies that it is not. Whatever fiscal pressure the district is absorbing right now — zero fund balance, negative reserve accounts, $8-9 million in structural gap — the development pipeline is not a relief valve for this budget cycle or the next two.

The school board vacancy going to November rather than an immediate appointment lands differently after four months of tracking this governance structure. A seven-month gap at District 5 during the most acute budget year in recent memory is not standard administrative sequencing. It is a choice — plausible on process grounds, uncomfortable in context. The distinction matters: the council had discretion, used it in a particular direction, and David is noting the outcome without imputing bad faith to the method.

Councilor West's VOC amendment is the one clean action of the evening. She took specific regulatory language from a public commenter, turned it into enforceable plan text, and got unanimous agreement including from the two dissenting votes on the overall plan. That is how evidence moves into governance. It happened once in a four-hour meeting.

The Chamber speaker named the school-to-tax-base connection explicitly. No staff member did. No councilor picked it up. The thread David opened in January — whether the council would produce integrated household tax burden analysis before major votes — remains open. Tonight is additional evidence it will not happen organically.
