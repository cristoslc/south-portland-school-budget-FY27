# Cumulative Record: Rachel (PERSONA-008)
## Meeting: City Council Regular Meeting -- April 14, 2026 -- 2026-04-14

### Interpretation

She came to this meeting because the March 19 consent calendar put the school budget workshop here, and because seventeen weeks have trained her to show up even when the agenda looks routine. What she did not come expecting was the clarification that reorganizes the map she has been working from since December.

The City Council cannot stop a school closure. That decision belongs entirely to the school board.

She has been at city council meetings since December 1st. She tracked the Mahoney project from $194 million down to a deferred remainder. She listened to Councilor Matthews say "closing schools" and "more like eight" in subordinate clauses that no one followed up on. She watched the February 10 guidance workshop set the tax ceiling that constrained everything downstream. She understood, at least functionally, that the council was upstream of the board on money. What she did not have made explicit until tonight is that the school board is the only body that decides whether a building closes — and no council vote, no referendum outcome, no amount of taxpayer pressure at a city hall podium changes that. She has not been attending the wrong meetings. But she has been carrying an imprecise map of where the authority actually lives.

The numbers the hearing surfaces — 42 teachers, 23% enrollment drop — she has been carrying since December 8th. They land differently now not because they are new but because they are being transmitted formally up the chain, embedded in a budget document the council will vote on May 5th and voters will decide on June 9th. Eight weeks. The fund balance is gone, so there is no slow-walk option. The math is not going to improve on its own between now and then.

What she cannot stop thinking about on the drive home is the specific danger in that timeline. A budget can go to voters in eight weeks with assumptions that require a school to close without naming the school. She does not know whether the budget passed April 7th does this. She could not get a straight answer tonight. What she knows is that a yes vote on June 9th could ratify an arithmetic that points at a specific building, and the family in that building might not find out until after the vote is already done.

The school board is the only place this gets decided. April 29th is the next moment that matters. She is already texting.
