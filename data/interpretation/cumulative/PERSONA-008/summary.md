# Cumulative Summary: Rachel (PERSONA-008)

### Current Understanding

The reconfiguration is no longer a threat — it is a vote with a timestamp. On March 30 the board closed Kaler Elementary 5-1 and passed Option A 4-2, restructuring every elementary family in the district: all PreK-1 students to Dyer and Small, all grades 2-4 to Brown and Skillin, effective September 2026. The institution spent four months moving from ceremonial silence to casual use of "closing schools" as vocabulary, and the $7.2 million deficit Rachel first heard in December has grown to $8.4 million. The administration presented a formal proposal with no cost slide, no attendance boundaries, no transportation modeling, and no specific IEP transition plan — then voted it through anyway.

One important shift arrived on April 13: the board chair announced a formal April 29 vote to delay the September 2026 timeline, citing "strong support." But board members immediately clarified this is "a change in pace," not a halt to reconfiguration itself. Kaler's closure is financially confirmed as irreversible. A Reconfiguration Committee is forming. Brown Elementary's principal is resigning in June. The FY27 budget passed 4-2 on April 7 and is now advancing toward a June 9 referendum.

Rachel learned on April 14 that the city council has no authority over school closures — that power rests entirely with the school board. After eighteen weeks of tracking two governing bodies, she now knows the fight lives at the board alone. The administration has publicly acknowledged that listening sessions should have preceded the vote. Staff morale sits at 92% negative. A recall petition has been filed. The fund balance is depleted. Her daughter still does not know which school she will attend in September, and the answer has been unavailable since February 4. Rachel is no longer trying to prevent the reconfiguration — she is trying to make its implementation accountable, humane, and documented.
