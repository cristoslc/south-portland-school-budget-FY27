# Cumulative Record: Rachel (PERSONA-008)
## Meeting: School Board Regular Meeting -- April 13, 2026 -- 2026-04-13

### Interpretation

She has learned, over seventeen weeks, to clock how fast the chair speaks at the top of a meeting. When DeAngelos says the most important thing in the first five minutes — before routine business, before the pledge — Rachel knows the institution is trying to get ahead of something. What it is getting ahead of is the anger that has been building since March 30. What it offers in exchange is a pause: there will be a vote on April 29 to delay the September reconfiguration, and there is "strong support" for it.

Rachel felt it. The relief was real. She texted people. For the first time in seventeen weeks, a sentence came out of that room that moved in her direction.

But she sat there for three hours, and she knows how to listen past the headline now. The Reconfiguration Committee is forming this month — Smith and Richardson as board representatives — which means the planning for wherever this ends up is already active. And then Holman said it near the end, clearly, for the record: "I don't envisage any halt. I think we're talking about a change in pace." DeAngelos confirmed that the September date is what is being delayed, not the reconfiguration itself.

*Change in pace.* That is the sentence she is going home with.

She registered the Kaler confirmation differently than she expected. Ketchen saying "I really don't think so" about keeping it open, assigning it $5.7 million in the location analysis — it amounts to official closing of a door Rachel knew was already closed. What she felt for the Kaler families in that room is something she recognizes from the other side: the particular anguish of people who showed up and got told the hard thing without being told what comes next.

Because what comes next is still unanswered for everyone. Which grades go to which buildings. What the zones look like. Whether a family with kids in two age bands navigates two buildings, two start times, two drop-offs. A Brown parent named something tonight she has been unable to articulate as precisely: three transitions in three years for a kindergartner under any version of this plan. Nobody on the board offered a concrete answer.

And Beth Kellogg, Brown Elementary's principal, is resigning effective June 30. Brown is likely to be a receiving school. It now also has a leadership vacancy. Rachel notes that and says nothing yet.

The April 29 vote is the new March 30. She is already organizing for it.

### Emotional Register

Rachel leaves with a cautious relief she does not quite know what to do with — the delay announcement is real, but "change of pace" has already been filed in the same mental drawer as "fabulous" and "no public referendum," and she is texting people about April 29 before she reaches the parking lot.
