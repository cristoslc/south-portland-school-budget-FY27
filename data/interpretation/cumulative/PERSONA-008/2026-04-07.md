# Cumulative Record: Rachel (PERSONA-008)
## Meeting: School Board Regular Meeting -- April 7, 2026 -- 2026-04-07

### Interpretation

The budget passes four to two and the reconfiguration is now embedded in the financial record of the district. That's the headline. But the moment Rachel will be describing to parents for weeks is the exchange that happened before the vote: Richardson asks what reconfiguration actually costs, and Prince says she didn't realize the board wanted a presentation on that tonight. No slide prepared. For the months of meetings, the nine scenarios, the five-hour workshops — nobody made a slide with a cost figure the board could look at before voting to send this budget to the city council. The $75,000 moving budget and $179,000 contingency are numbers without a defense. A parent brought data to the microphone: peer districts overspent transportation by 20% during comparable reconfigurations. Nobody challenged it. The vote happened anyway.

What the chair asks about attendance zones — into the record, as a formal board question — and what she receives in return is the answer Rachel has been watching the district not give since February 4th. "That's a detail we do not have at this point." It has been ten weeks since the administration stood at a forum and admitted they hadn't modeled transportation impacts. The vote is done. The budget has passed. The answer is still: we don't know. A parent asked whether start times would be staggered for families with kids at two different buildings. The administration said they hadn't considered it.

What the administration did say — and what Rachel files as one of the only honest moments in months of process — is that a public speaker was right: the listening sessions should have happened before the vote, not after. Prince said so directly. That acknowledgment changes nothing structural. But it is the first time anyone in authority has confirmed in plain language what Rachel has known since January: the engagement was sequenced as a post-decision courtesy, not a genuine input mechanism.

Richardson and Feller vote no again. The 92% staff morale figure validates what Richardson said aloud on March 30 — "very low staff morale" was not a board member's impression, it was a survey finding waiting to be published. The two reinstated teachers are real, one-year-only positions with no school assignment yet, and Rachel registers them as real even as she understands they change nothing about where her daughter is going in September.

The recall petition filed at the meeting is a new variable she doesn't know what to do with yet. Someone in that room believes there is still a legal mechanism worth trying. She is not ready to count on it. But she is not ready to dismiss it either.
