# Cumulative Record: Linda (PERSONA-007)
## Meeting: School Board Regular Meeting -- April 7, 2026 -- 2026-04-07

### Interpretation

The vote is what I needed, and the vote is what I got. Four-two, budget advancing to city council as the board's proposal — thin, with two legitimate no votes on the record, but enough to walk into the April 14 council meeting with something to defend. After March 30's 5-2 failure and the particular exhaustion of watching the same last-minute-change pattern that sank us at the Zeal vote in December produce another fractured result, getting any majority across the line felt like moving a boulder.

What I will actually be thinking about between now and April 14 is Meredith Diamond. She stood at the public comment microphone and read FY25 audit findings into the record: $2.37 million overspent without authorization, $400,000 in grant funds absorbed into general fund, $123,000 repaid to the state, $600,000 in benefit under-withholding the district had to absorb. Nobody at the table responded. Abigail completed the honest accounting on April 2 with the systematic under-budgeting analysis — that was the moment I marked the root cause narrative as finally public and deployable. But the compliance findings Diamond cited tonight are a different category. They're not structural patterns; they're specific transactions and specific failures of financial control. I need to know when those findings were formally presented to this board and what corrective action was taken. That is a finance committee question before we vote the warrant articles, and I'm not letting it go.

The insurance correction deserves its own honest recognition. Five-point-one-four percent instead of 11.5 percent — $655,000 freed, with a specific allocation proposal on the table before discussion even began. More importantly, Abigail finally named what we should have built years ago: an actual unearmarked contingency line. The admission embedded in that framing — that accounts labeled "contingency" in prior budgets were always earmarked — sits alongside the disclosure that existing reserve accounts are in deficit from years of untracked expenditures. The financial infrastructure was hollower than even the March 23 and April 2 testimony captured.

The OT situation worries me on a different axis entirely — not budget mechanics, but IEP compliance with legal exposure attached. An ed tech described working nearly the full year short-staffed in a functional life skills classroom with no subs and no extra pay, while students with IEPs were in the room. There are no candidates on the recall list for the replacement positions. "Warrants more stakeholder conversation" is not a plan when a parent's attorney is asking whether their child received prescribed services.

We have a budget. It is not finished. Richardson and Feller put legitimate things on the record. But we have a budget, and the clock to April 14 is running.
