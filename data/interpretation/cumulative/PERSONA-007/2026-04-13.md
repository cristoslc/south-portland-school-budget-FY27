# Cumulative Record: Linda (PERSONA-007)
## Meeting: School Board Regular Meeting -- April 13, 2026 -- 2026-04-13

### Interpretation

The night before the city council presentation, and we still don't know what our current-year books close at. That is the sentence I keep returning to. The FY26 deficit range is $80,000 to $1.1 million — a spread so wide it barely functions as information — and sitting on top of it is a SPTA time-beyond-contract liability that nobody can quantify because the submission process rolled out last week and May is the earliest we'll have a figure. Holman was right to surface the finance committee notation publicly. But the fact that it was sitting there in yellow on monthly reports while we were building the FY27 asks is the exact failure mode Abigail documented on April 2: costs that accumulate outside the tracked budget until they can't be ignored. I know this pattern. I named it at December 8. I'm watching it one more time.

What I'm marking as genuine progress is the location analysis. Abigail clarified the Kaylor cost in a way that ended a confusion that had been degrading public and board conversations for weeks. Roughly $4 million in net impact, approximately half of all FY27 reductions — those are numbers people can reason about. And when she was asked directly whether there's a financially defensible path to keeping Kaylor open, she said no. Clear, on the record. That is the kind of institutional honesty that took this district until March 23 to start producing consistently. I'll take it.

The reconfiguration delay to 2027-28 is the right call, and I don't need to pretend otherwise. Since March 2, I've been carrying the implementation feasibility question — no RACI, no attendance zones, no redistricting plan before the April 29 vote — and it never had a satisfying answer. A year's delay means 2027-28 implementation with real planning time behind it. That's better governance than a rushed September execution that falls apart on the details.

What worries me most going into April 14 is the cumulative ask. The $360K shared services offset is new ground — SROs are a service we've always funded ourselves — and the 1% contingency increase is the structural fix I've wanted since December. Both are legitimate. But the council is about to hear them together the morning after a meeting where we couldn't close our FY26 books. I need to walk into that chamber ready to explain why we're asking for more when we still don't know what we owe.

Beth Kellogg's resignation at Brown is a separate kind of problem: a principal search during the planning year for reconfiguration, with Kaylor families in active grief and April 29 still ahead. The administrative bandwidth question is real and I don't yet have an answer for it.

Abigail's FY28 signal — "careful planning required" — is the line I'm writing down. It means this year's work is the floor, not the ceiling.
