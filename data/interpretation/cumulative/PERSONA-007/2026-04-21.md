# Cumulative Record: Linda (PERSONA-007)
## Meeting: 2026-04-21-city-council -- 2026-04-21

### Interpretation

The consent calendar was ninety seconds. Adrian's resignation, the seat going to November, and we moved on. I understand the charter logic — the appointee would have served until November anyway — but I keep turning it over. Six weeks from a validation referendum, six sitting board members instead of seven. That's not a procedural emergency, but it's one missed meeting away from a quorum problem at the worst possible moment. The council chose the path that preserves democratic legitimacy over governance continuity, and I can't argue with the principle. I can note the timing.

The rest of the night belonged to the comprehensive plan. Four hours. I was in that room, and I was also running through budget emails on my phone during the Bug Light debate, which tells you something about where my head is six weeks out from June 9.

What I'll carry out of this meeting is Thomas O'Boyle's language. The Chamber of Commerce advocacy director stood up and named school staffing, school facilities, and the cost-shifting problem — explicitly, on the record, in front of the full council. That framing is something I've been trying to build for months. It's in the transcript now. The problem is the timeline attached to it: Nevajda laid out the comp plan's fiscal payoff honestly — three-plus years of zoning work, then a construction lag, then revenue. That's a 5-to-8-year runway before any of this registers in a budget. O'Boyle's argument is correct and structurally irrelevant to FY27.

The 5-2 vote worries me less for what it decided than for what it signals. A mayor on the losing side of a major land-use vote, a council running past 11 PM, visible fractures on the environmental amendment — that's a governing body with its own internal tensions heading into May 5. I need that council present and coherent when they vote our budget. Right now they spent their April bandwidth on a 15-year horizon document while the June 9 referendum went completely unmentioned. I've seen this pattern. December 1 was the last night we pretended everything was fine. Tonight wasn't pretending — the council is simply focused elsewhere. That's a different problem, and in some ways harder to move.
