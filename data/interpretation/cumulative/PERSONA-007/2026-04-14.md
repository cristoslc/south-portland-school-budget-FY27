# Cumulative Record: Linda (PERSONA-007)
## Meeting: City Council Regular Meeting -- April 14, 2026 -- 2026-04-14

### Interpretation

This is the moment the year has been building toward, and we arrive one week late with our tardiness on record. The city manager's position paper documents it plainly: the school board had not adopted a budget by the April 7 hearing date. I know all the reasons — the 5-2 failure on March 30, the December pattern of last-minute changes fracturing votes, the legitimate objections from Richardson and Feller that needed to be worked through before anyone could say yes. None of that context is in the position paper. What's in the position paper is the fact. This is the same governance failure mode I have watched since December 10, and now it has a citation in a city document before I've said a word in defense of the budget.

What I held onto is the legal framework. The city manager's position paper got the boundary right: council can move the bottom line, but they cannot tell us which building to keep open, which positions to restore, which line items to change. I've been watching for that boundary since February 10, when Rosemarie had to explain to the room that the council's "do your best" approach was insufficient. Having it in writing before public comment opens is protective. The "guidance" format is still the unscripted variable. I read every face in that chamber for where the majority is sitting after each comment. That is not anxiety — that is governance.

What the council needs to absorb, and what the position paper alone cannot convey, is the structural story underneath our per-pupil cost. We are running at 20% state subsidy coverage against a 55% EPS formula expectation. South Portland's $26,651 per-pupil cost is the highest among comparable districts — and I know exactly how that figure will be used in the validation campaign. But you cannot compare our cost to peer districts while ignoring that our state subsidy is running at less than half the formula target. The 78 positions this budget eliminates are not a management choice made in a vacuum. Augusta's numbers are in that gap too, and no councilor can vote on Augusta's share.

The validation clock is the pressure I carry out of this chamber. May 5 council vote. June 9 referendum. Whatever guidance emerges tonight, we have to decide whether to act on it and still arrive at a warrant vote that holds. There is no fund balance to absorb a council-directed reduction. That is not a negotiating position — it is the fiscal floor.
