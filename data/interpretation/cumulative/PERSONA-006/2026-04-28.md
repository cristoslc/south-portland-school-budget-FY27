# Cumulative Record: Tom (PERSONA-006)
## Meeting: City Council Budget Workshop #2 -- April 28, 2026 -- 2026-04-28

### Interpretation

The April 28 workshop is where the asks get added up, and the addition is not going Tom's way. He already has the enrollment numbers, the per-pupil cost, and the 78 positions memorized since December. What he didn't have was this: the parking lot items from the first two workshops total $1.28 million, which puts another 1.4 percentage points on top of the city-side baseline. If the council funds everything in the parking lot, the city tax rate increase alone goes from 5.1% to 6.5%. That is before the school side. Before the sewer. Before November.

The fund balance forgiveness is the thing Tom is watching most closely, and the answer is already taking shape before anyone has formally voted. The school is asking the council to forgive $1 to $1.5 million in overage — the district's own lower estimate of a shortfall the city had pegged as high as $3 million. The city manager and finance staff said no: forgive it and the city either defers capital work it had planned to fund from reserves, or raises property taxes to replace the money. That is the correct analysis. It is the first time all winter that Tom has heard city administration say something he agrees with from inside city hall.

And then: a majority of councilors have already signaled support for granting some forgiveness before the vote.

Tom has watched this council hold on the eviction moratorium in February. He watched it say the right words on Mahoney and then set no ceiling. He watched it draw down the reserve fund twice in back-to-back weeks in March while 72 school employees were carrying layoff notices home. The pattern is legible by now. When cost-shifting carries political weight, the council bends toward it. City staff said no. The majority is pointing yes anyway. The final vote is May 12. Tom is not optimistic — not because the council is hiding anything, but because he has watched this script run to the same conclusion enough times to recognize the opening.

The per-pupil cost, the enrollment collapse, the staffing growth — these are now the official framing in the council's own budget materials. Tom has been carrying these numbers since December. What changes at April 28 is not the data. It is that the council is now the decision-maker, and the decisions are forming before the meeting ends.

### Emotional Register

Tom feels a cold, pre-emptive resignation — he walked into the workshop hoping the council would behave like city staff, and the majority is already pointing in the other direction, absorbing the school's overage at the expense of capital reserves or future taxes while the final vote is still two weeks away.
