# Cumulative Record: Tom (PERSONA-006)
## Meeting: School Board Regular Meeting -- April 13, 2026 -- 2026-04-13

### Interpretation

Tom watched the 6% ceiling survive April 7 and thought he finally had a number to carry to June. Seven days later, the board voted unanimously to go back to the city council and ask for more. An additional 1% on top of the ceiling to seed the fund balance. Another $360,000 for SRO officers and snow removal absorbed by the city. If the council grants it, the effective tax impact moves to roughly 7%, and the $257 figure he has been protecting since February is no longer the number on the ballot. He has watched this pattern so many times it barely surprises him — but unanimously is new. That's every member of this board, including the ones who spent months arguing for fiscal restraint, voting to ask the city to exceed the ceiling the city set.

The current-year picture is bad and getting worse. The finance director confirmed a FY26 deficit in the range of $80,000 to $1.1 million, with benefits accounts unreconciled for years still being worked through. And then the SPTA contract disclosure: hours worked above fifteen per week haven't been calculated or paid yet. Staff are just now submitting figures. So that deficit range is still moving. The district has nothing left to absorb any of it. The backstop is the city's undesignated fund balance — the same reserve drawn down twice in back-to-back weeks in March, the same one Tom has been watching erode as his one signal of sound municipal management. It is now structurally committed to cleaning up the school department's current-year books.

The finance director herself is doing honest work. Her location-sorted budget showed per-school spending ranging from $5.7 million to $7.8 million, with a public commenter noting that implies per-pupil costs as high as $38,000 at one school versus $28,000 at another. She flagged FY28 risks explicitly and said holding under 6% next year will require careful planning. That's the most direct acknowledgment Tom has heard from anyone at the administration table that the FY28 math doesn't work in steady state.

Two residents said what Tom thinks. Ed Cobb did the ten-year compound calculation at 6% annually: the average bill hits $10,000 in less than a decade. Mindy Allos spent sixteen months on a district steering committee — not an outside agitator — and told the board directly that South Portland is becoming unaffordable and they should stop asking for more. The board heard both of them. Then voted unanimously to ask for more.
