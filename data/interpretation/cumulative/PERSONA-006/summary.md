# Cumulative Summary: Tom (PERSONA-006)

### Current Understanding

Tom is a South Portland homeowner with a sustained focus on fiscal impacts to property taxpayers. As of April 14, his working picture of the budget situation is as follows.

The school district faces an $8.4M deficit. The FY27 budget passed the school board 4-2 on April 7, holding to the city council's 6% local levy ceiling — a figure that translates to approximately $257/year on a home assessed at the city average of $514,000. The day before the city council received that budget, the board voted unanimously to ask the council for an additional 1% levy increase plus $360,000 in cost absorption, pushing the effective ask toward 7%. The crisis has documented roots: a 23% elementary enrollment drop (1,401 to 1,080 students) while 82 positions were added; per-pupil cost of $26,651, the highest among comparable districts; the fund balance drawn from approximately $5.9M in FY23 to zero; two consecutive years of budget non-compliance; seven finance directors in six years; multi-year under-budgeting of known cost lines; and $2.37M in FY24-25 expenditures that a public commenter cited from the FY25 audit as made without board authorization. The state covers approximately 20% of education costs against a 55% statutory obligation. Labor contracts structurally grow costs beyond 6% per year automatically, making the 6% ceiling mathematically untenable in steady state for FY28. The FY26 school year is projected to end in deficit, with the city's undesignated fund balance now committed as the explicit backstop.

Three additional pressures are simultaneously active: the Pearl Street sewer upgrade (22% annual rate increases for three consecutive years, raising the average bill from ~$56 to ~$90/month, structured as revenue bonds requiring no referendum); an $87M police and fire bond targeting a November 2026 referendum; and the Mahoney City Center at $70-76M in stripped-down scope, targeting a 2027 referendum.
