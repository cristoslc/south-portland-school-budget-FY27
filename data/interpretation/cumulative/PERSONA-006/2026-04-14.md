# Cumulative Record: Tom (PERSONA-006)
## Meeting: City Council Regular Meeting -- April 14, 2026 -- 2026-04-14

### Interpretation

Tom has been carrying every number in that April 14 presentation since December. The 23% enrollment drop, the 82 added positions, the $26,651 per-pupil cost, the 20 cents on the dollar from Augusta — none of it is new. He internalized it at workshops, confirmed it at public forums, and has been repeating it to his neighbor Bob since winter. What April 14 is is a procedural threshold: the school budget has formally arrived on the city council's table. And it arrives exactly one day after the school board voted unanimously to ask the council to exceed the ceiling the council set.

That sequence is what this meeting really means. The city council handed down 3-6% guidance in February. Tom called that the first moment since December that someone with actual budget authority stood up for taxpayers. The school board passed a 4-2 budget on April 7 that held to 6%. Then, twenty-four hours before the council received it, that same board turned around unanimously and asked for 7% plus $360,000 in additional cost absorption. Tom watched the board pass a compliant budget with one hand and undermine it with the other before the ink dried.

Now the council has to decide. That is the only unresolved question at this meeting that matters to Tom, and the presentation doesn't answer it. He sits through per-pupil comparisons he has had memorized since Feller read them aloud on March 30, enrollment graphs he first saw in December, and a fund balance slide he has been describing as zero since February. The state funding failure — 20 cents where the formula says 55 — still burns. He has been waiting for someone to go to Augusta about it all winter, and nobody has. The cost keeps landing on his property tax bill while Augusta calls South Portland a rich community and shortchanges the formula.

What Tom is watching for at this meeting is the council's posture. The February guidance was the one enforceable thing this entire winter produced. The board has now formally asked to exceed it. Tom has watched the council say the right words and then fold on the TIF, on Mahoney, on the fund balance draws. He has also watched it hold — on the moratorium, on bikes, on the February guidance itself. He does not know yet which version of the council shows up for this decision. That is the only live question April 14 leaves him with.
