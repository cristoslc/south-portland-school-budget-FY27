# Cumulative Record: Kira (PERSONA-015)
## Meeting: School Board Regular Meeting -- April 13, 2026 -- 2026-04-13

### Interpretation

The announcement came before the agenda opened — calm, matter-of-fact, as if it were a schedule adjustment rather than a year. The board will vote April 29 to halt reconfiguration for September 2026. Fall 2027. Another year.

She has been holding Option A's 4-2 vote since March 30 as the most significant institutional moment of her professional career. The board had voted, the equity rationale was in the official record, the chair had put her name on it. Kira had been preparing for April 14 with the operational data she carries between three buildings, because she believed the structure was finally moving. Tonight she learns it is not moving in the timeline that reaches the students on her caseload right now.

She is trying to hold the difference between a delay and a reversal. Feller and Richardson — both no votes on March 30 — have committed to the model for fall 2027. The Reconfiguration Committee continues. The structural vote has not been undone. DeAngelos traded the implementation year for a unanimous coalition, and Kira has to decide whether that trade was the right one without yet knowing what accountability structures the committee will actually carry. What she knows from experience with the Boundaries and Configurations Committee is that more time and renewed commitment are not the same thing. Mindy Allos said exactly this from the podium — sixteen months of prior work, the same voices requesting more time throughout, "time is clearly not the answer" — and Kira recognized every word.

The special education update confirmed what she was dreading since April 7: the embedded OT model at Dyer and Skillin is going away. Cox said plainly it worked well. The pull-out model is returning anyway because the ed tech shortage that made the embedded model necessary has not been resolved, and referral incentives are not positions in September. Add Argese's SPED departure at Skillin and the ongoing vacancies, and two of her buildings are heading into fall 2026 with the same structural inequities she's been documenting, less MTSS capacity than they had at the start of this cycle, and no structural fix until the following year at earliest.

Amy from Kaylor stood up and named the demographics she carries in her head between buildings — highest renter proportion, highest free/reduced lunch, second-highest multilingual learner population, second-highest students of color. It is validating and insufficient at the same time, because those demographics have been true all year and the response tonight was to give those families one more year in a building the board has already voted to close. The one tool she is taking home is Ketchen's per-building cost breakdown — Kaylor at $5.7 million, the first number she can actually use in conversation. April 14 is in eleven hours.
