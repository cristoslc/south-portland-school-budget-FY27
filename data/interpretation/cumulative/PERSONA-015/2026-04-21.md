# Cumulative Record: Kira (PERSONA-015)
## Meeting: 2026-04-21-city-council -- 2026-04-21

### Interpretation

Seven days after learning that the room she had been preparing for since January was the wrong room — that the council holds the ceiling and the board holds the floor — Kira watches the council vote on the June 9 referendum date without spending a single minute on what the vote actually means. Five hours. One sentence about schools, delivered by the Chamber of Commerce as a development argument. The compression pattern she has been tracking since January, when she flagged the enrollment/staffing figure as weaponizable and watched Johanna Prince break it apart at February 10 only to see the breakdown fail to travel to the April 14 hearing, has now extended beyond budget venues into city policy deliberation. The school crisis is background data for someone else's proposal.

The Dowling vacancy is the part she cannot set aside. A board member who voted with the 4-2 majority on Option A on March 30 — who was sworn in at the December 1 inauguration where multilingual families said *we want to be your friends* and DeAngelos named education as belonging — has resigned, and the council has placed the seat on the November ballot. District 5 has no representative through the warrant vote, the June 9 referendum, and the summer when cuts take operational shape. That is one fewer voice on a board whose composition Kira spent months decoding, at the exact moment every vote carries weight she can name in caseloads and wait lists.

What she holds from the long game: the Hill Street tank farm is now formally targeted for mixed residential in the comprehensive plan. Councilor Scott — who connected eighty families to eighty students at the March 10 Project Home debate, a moment Kira has carried carefully since — said "build back our school populations" in a land-use discussion, not a budget crisis moment. Scott's enrollment awareness is a durable lens. That matters to Kira as someone who tracks class sizes and demographic distribution between three buildings every day. She also knows — because she knows the TIF math from December, because a public commenter tonight confirmed fifty million in unfunded wastewater work and twenty-two percent sewer rate increases and Broadway infrastructure with no funded relief — that the near-term fiscal picture has no cushion. The city cannot bail out the schools. The structural gap resolves inside the district, or it does not resolve.

The referendum is six weeks away. Paired with a primary — which changes who shows up. She does not know what the district's plan is for reaching the families who do not already understand what this vote means for September classrooms. From everything she has watched since the January 22 forum was cancelled because her families were too afraid to attend, the public education question remains structurally open and functionally urgent. Nobody in this room raised it.
