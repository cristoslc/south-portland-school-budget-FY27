# Cumulative Record: Kira (PERSONA-015)
## Meeting: City Council Regular Meeting -- April 14, 2026 -- 2026-04-14

### Interpretation

She has been preparing for April 14 since January 6, when the city manager first named it as the meeting where the school department would present to the council. She marked it in her calendar the way you mark the date you find out whether you got the grant — not with hope exactly, but with the specific dread of someone who knows the outcome will be significant regardless of which direction it goes. She went in with cross-building data. She left with the clearest structural fact she has had in four and a half months: the council can only move the bottom line. Not the ed tech positions. Not the MTSS slots. Not the question of which building keeps a full-time interventionist after reconfiguration. The board holds all of that. The council holds the number.

That is clarifying in a way she did not expect. The participation barrier she has been documenting since December 4 — the one that kept her families out of forums, that the city manager confirmed was eating its own remedies by February, that has suppressed community voice at every venue that matters — is most consequential not here but at the board level. Every parent who fights their way to a council meeting to protect a single school is fighting in the wrong building. The decision architecture puts equity accountability where it has always been: on the people who vote on which positions and which programs and which buildings.

What she cannot absorb cleanly is the 16 ed techs named in an official inter-governmental document for the first time. She has known that number since January 12. But there is something different about watching it move from a school board slide to the floor of a city council hearing — watching the people who sit next to kids on her caseload at three different buildings become a number in a governmental proceeding rather than a fact she carries alone between parking lots. And the 42 teachers still have no breakdown. She does not know which of those forty-two are specialists, which are itinerant, whether any of them are her. Nobody said.

The enrollment/staffing narrative arrived at the council level without equity disaggregation, exactly as she feared in January when she flagged it as weaponizable. Enrollment minus 300, staffing plus 82. Johanna Prince broke that number apart at February 10 — the ed techs, the IEP students, the multilingual learners, the McKinney-Vento youth — and the breakdown is in the council's own record from two months ago. It did not appear to travel to this hearing. She is the person with the cross-building data to complicate that number. The forum she was preparing to use it in cannot act on it anyway.
