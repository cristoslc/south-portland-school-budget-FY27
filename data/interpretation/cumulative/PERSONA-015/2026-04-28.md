# Cumulative Record: Kira (PERSONA-015)
## Meeting: 2026-04-28-city-council -- 2026-04-28

### Interpretation

One week after watching the council vote on the June 9 referendum date without spending a single minute on what the vote means, Kira reads the April 28 budget workshop materials and finds the city manager's office recommending the council grant zero fund balance forgiveness. Zero. The argument is that forgiving the school's overage delays capital projects or raises city taxes, and it lands as an accusation dressed in spreadsheets — as if the structural deficit was self-inflicted rather than driven by health insurance climbing twelve percent and a state that covers twenty percent of costs instead of fifty-five. Seven days ago she noted the institutional silence pattern extending to the meeting that set the referendum date itself. Tonight the silence is replaced by a formal staff recommendation that would make the cuts deeper.

But the materials also contain the most useful piece of math she has held since December: the gap the school needs the council to bridge is $1 to $1.5 million, not the $3 million the city had been quoting. The number is smaller than feared, and a council majority already told the room at April 14 they would forgive something. That is not nothing. That is the first downward revision of the fiscal threat she has read since January, attached to a council posture that exists in the official record. She is holding both the city manager's recommended zero and the council's earlier stated willingness and trying to add them together.

The 16 ed techs and 42 teachers are now in formal budget materials before the council, and the material includes a specific fact she has been asking for since January: the 78 positions represent twelve percent of all district staff. That proportion gives her a new frame for the scope of the damage — not just headcount, but density. And the budget documents still do not publicly specify which teaching positions are among the forty-two. Specialists. Interventionists. Itinerant staff. Her own role. The breakdown she has been tracking through every meeting since January 12, through every principal report that never surfaced cross-building data, through the March 23 SPTA cut list that named the four MTSS positions but not which teachers, remains absent from the document the council will use to decide what relief to grant. May 12 is the final workshop. She is going.
