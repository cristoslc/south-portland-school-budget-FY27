# Cumulative Summary: Jaylen (PERSONA-012)

### Current Understanding

As of April 14, Jaylen holds a complete architectural understanding of the budget crisis and its institutional constraints. The FY27 budget passed 4-2 on April 7 with cuts largely intact. Seventy-two pink slips were issued March 18. The deficit has grown to $8.4M from the $7.2M baseline established in December. The budget calendar runs to a May 5 city council vote and a June 9 community referendum — both legally limited to the total bottom-line number. Under state law and the city charter, no formal body in the approval chain can direct which specific programs survive.

At SPHS, seven positions were disclosed in a five-minute workshop presentation on March 23: two science, one English, one World Language/ESOL, two special education case managers, one social work, and one CTE position. Art (0.5 FTE) is confirmed cut; Hannah Schultz is the named educator. The co-curricular stipend line is cut separately by $27,630. The evening shuttle is reduced to a single city loop, threatening after-school athletics for students without private transportation. Theater has not appeared in any official cut document; Angela Kabesa's student report confirming the spring 2026 production of Chicago is the strongest affirmative evidence available.

The crisis is compound: structural (the state funds roughly 20% of costs against a 55% formula), managerial (34 audit entries, illegal overspending, six finance directors in five years), and institutional (the city's austerity posture was forming in closed sessions before the school board presented its case, and the February 10 guidance was solicited by Chair DeAngelis, not imposed from outside). The budget and immigration enforcement crises affect overlapping and in some cases identical families. Student engagement mechanisms — from public forums to formal board roles — have been systematically limited or ornamental. Teachers organized independent Augusta advocacy and secured approximately $300,000 in additional state funding, demonstrating the state floor is not fixed. The public comment microphone remains the only real seat in the process — and it cannot, by law or practice, direct the outcome for any specific program.
