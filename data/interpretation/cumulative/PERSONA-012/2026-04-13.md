# Cumulative Record: Jaylen (PERSONA-012)
## Meeting: School Board Regular Meeting -- April 13, 2026 -- 2026-04-13

### Interpretation

Angela names Chicago at 9:25 in the transcript, and Jaylen knows that show. He was in it. He knows what it cost in rehearsal time and what it meant to do it well, and hearing his student rep call it "amazing" in that chamber is something real. It is also ten seconds in three hours.

The $18.2 million is the whole high school. That is what the finance director's location-based breakdown looks like when SPHS appears: one number, one line, no interior. He has spent four months trying to find specific programs inside budget categories that don't map to anything he experiences. April 13 confirms that even in a building-by-building presentation, the high school is a single figure. Everything he needs to know about whether AP Chem has a teacher in September, whether his coach's stipend survives, whether the theater position is in the FY27 headcount — it is all inside $18.2 million and nobody at the board table asked what was in there.

The $360,000 vote is what he is carrying out. He has been watching the budget crisis framed as inevitability since December — structural gap, depleted reserves, no room, every cut a function of not enough money. Then April 13 puts $360,000 on the table: a potential city cost-share for SRO and snow removal. Richardson and Holman want to restore teachers. Related arts at the middle school. The positions they have been naming since March. Three board members vote for the savings account. Three to two. The board majority, offered a chance to use available money to restore something they cut, chose to bank it. He is careful not to read bad faith into a decision that may reflect legitimate fiscal caution, but he cannot pretend the vote says nothing. It says something about priorities. The frame of fiscal necessity does not fully account for it.

The FY28 warning is the thing that won't leave him. The finance director said "careful planning" for next year — that is the year he's trying to plan his senior courses around. He has spent four months focused on whether FY27 destroys his programs before fall 2026. Now the finance director is telling the room that fall 2027 will require the same management under the same 6% ceiling. Teacher Chloe Bartlett asked directly from the microphone whether next March would bring another year of fear and not knowing. The answer was a list of unknowns. He needed the same answer she did. Nobody gave it.

Coach Cahill's Portland Press Herald award is real. The track ceremony is genuine. He holds it next to the savings account vote and the FY28 warning and keeps going.
