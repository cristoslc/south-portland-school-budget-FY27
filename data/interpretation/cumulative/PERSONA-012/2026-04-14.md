# Cumulative Record: Jaylen (PERSONA-012)
## Meeting: City Council Regular Meeting -- April 14, 2026 -- 2026-04-14

### Interpretation

The school budget arrived at city hall on April 14 — a week late, because the board hadn't formally adopted a budget in time for the April 7 city council public hearing. He had watched the board vote 4-2 that same week at a hearing held inside his own school, where nobody said SPHS once in three hours and forty-nine minutes. The two institutions have been running on misaligned timelines since December 9. The misalignment now has a schedule. It also, it turns out, has a legal structure.

The legal constraint is the thing he didn't have before. Under state law and the city charter, the council can only vote on the school budget's total bottom-line number. Not programs. Not positions. Not theater or AP Chem or coaching stipends. The council that set the 4-6% guidance in February — the ceiling that made the specific cuts mathematically necessary — cannot now direct the district to undo any of them. They can increase or decrease the total, and that is the full extent of their formal authority. Jaylen has spent four months watching the city council as a powerful actor in this story: DeAngelis requesting guidance on February 3, the guidance arriving on February 10, the board constrained in the ways she had invited. That account is accurate. But April 14 gives him the full architecture. The council sets the ceiling. The board makes every specific decision. Voters approve or reject the sum. Nobody in that chain carries formal accountability for which programs disappear by name.

He still intends to go on May 5. He has been building toward that microphone since April 2. But he is precise now about what testifying can and cannot accomplish. If he stands up and says "don't cut theater," no council member can act on it — legally, structurally, they cannot. What they can act on is the total. The June 9 referendum works the same way: a binary question about the amount, not a conversation about what the amount contains. Fletcher's April 7 testimony — that organized advocacy was told it would not make a difference — described the school board stage. April 14 adds that at every subsequent stage, the mechanism is the same: only the total number is on the table.

The rest of the evening was the city clerk, social services, police, fire, the library, parks and recreation. He stopped being surprised by this in December. He takes notes anyway.
