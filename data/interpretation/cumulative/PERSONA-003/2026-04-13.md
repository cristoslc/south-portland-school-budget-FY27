# Cumulative Record: Jess (PERSONA-003)
## Meeting: School Board Regular Meeting -- April 13, 2026 -- 2026-04-13

### Interpretation

The first thing the chair said was essentially: we're not doing the reshuffle this September. And Jess felt the exhale before she could stop herself. After six weeks of watching structural decisions lock in one by one -- Option A, Kaler closing, the budget passing 4-2, layoff notices delivered before any public hearing -- something actually moved in the direction she needed. The board is voting April 29 to halt September 2026 implementation. There is still a process. The civic calendar is not purely ceremonial.

Then she kept watching.

Reconfiguration is delayed to fall 2027, not canceled. Mia starts kindergarten in 2028. The year the district reshuffles every elementary building will be the year before her daughter arrives. Jess has spent four months asking which door Mia walks through -- and the answer is now: a door that will have been remade the previous year, in a building still finding its footing, in a system still paying off the disruption. The question didn't resolve. It moved one year forward and got harder to see.

The Kaler closure is different from the other bad news. The finance director didn't say "the board voted and we're proceeding." She said: "I really don't think so. I'm very sorry." The apology is the thing. Someone who manages the numbers looked at them and felt sorry they couldn't be different. Jess has been reading district finances since December; she doesn't think the apology was performative. It also didn't change anything.

The Brown School parent named her situation a second time in two meetings. Last week it was Caroline Bowman -- the email list, the secondary click, no signal that a closure was happening. This week it was a dad at a birthday party, parents who didn't know, a kindergartner facing three school transitions in three years. Two consecutive meetings. Two community members describing Jess's exact position from the outside. The gap has been documented. It has not been addressed.

And then the teacher shaking at the podium asked the board whether next March would be another year of fear. The finance director's answer named "worldwide events" among the FY28 unknowns. The year the finance director is already flagging for careful planning is the year Mia starts kindergarten. Jess came into this meeting holding the reconfiguration delay as something real. She is leaving it knowing that the delay moved the disruption directly into her family's entry window, that the finance director is already worried about the year after that, and that the question of where her daughter goes to school cannot be answered by anyone in that room.
