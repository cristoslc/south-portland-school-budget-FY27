# Cumulative Summary: Jess (PERSONA-003)

### Current Understanding

As of mid-April 2026, Jess understands the South Portland school district as executing structural decisions that have outpaced the civic engagement calendar meant to accompany them. The deficit has grown from a potential $1.9 million in December 2025 to $8.4 million as cited in March 2026. Seventy-eight positions — 42 of them teachers — have been eliminated, with 72 layoff notices delivered March 18 before any public hearing or budget vote. The fund balance is depleted; the state covers roughly 20 percent of costs it is mandated to cover at 55 percent; the district has had seven finance directors in six years; and external audits found material reconciliation weaknesses and legally exceeded budget limits in consecutive years. The FY27 budget passed 4-2 and is now before the city council.

Reconfiguration — Option A, PreK-1 in two buildings and grades 2-4 in three — has been voted. Kaler Elementary is closed 6-1. September 2026 implementation is being halted pending an April 29 board vote; the new target is fall 2027, placing the transition in the year before Mia's kindergarten entry in 2028. Attendance boundaries have not been set and no building assignment is available for Mia. The finance director has already flagged FY28 as requiring careful planning given labor contract escalators, rising utilities, and new debt service.

Pre-K expansion, once Jess's most durable anchor, has softened. District-operated seats are flat at 64; expansion beyond that depends on external partnerships with income-eligibility or off-site placement constraints; a pre-K teacher FTE was cut from 4.0 to 3.0 in the FY27 budget; and the $171,000 EPS pre-K line in the budget is a pass-through that evaporates if the Head Start classroom is discontinued.

The city council is legally limited to voting on the school budget's bottom-line total and cannot direct specific positions or programs. The June 9 referendum is the only formal lever Jess can pull as a voter. A confirmed triple-plus tax stack — school increase, a Mahoney bond being scoped toward police and fire facilities, and sewer rate increases of approximately 22 percent annually for three years — has not been presented to residents as a combined household figure. The library's early childhood programming spaces, designed for Mia's age cohort, were removed from the Mahoney project scope with no replacement plan.
