# Cumulative Record: Jess (PERSONA-003)
## Meeting: School Board Regular Meeting -- April 7, 2026 -- 2026-04-07

### Interpretation

Four months of watching these meetings, and tonight I finally heard someone say my name. Not literally — Caroline Bowman doesn't know me — but she stood up during the second public comment period and described exactly my situation: a family whose kids aren't in school yet, who have no idea any of this is happening, who would have to be on a specific email list and then click a secondary link that doesn't even signal a school closure is under discussion to find out. She said it with a specificity that made me feel seen and sick at the same time. I almost missed all of this. I found out from a Facebook group.

The pre-K pass-through was the other thing I couldn't shake. The $171,000 line item for pre-K programming sounded like good news until the assistant superintendent explained it: if the Head Start classroom doesn't continue, the revenue disappears too. It's not an investment in early childhood — it's a balanced accounting entry. The pre-K thread I've been holding since January as my reason for staying is not a commitment. It's contingent on a program the district has not confirmed it will continue.

There was one moment that felt different. When someone asked why the district is keeping the Kaylor building rather than selling it, the superintendent mentioned "obligations around three and four-year-olds" and something about taking over child development services. That could be significant for a family with a toddler. But the description was brief, no one followed up, and the superintendent acknowledged there is no state roadmap for this transition at South Portland's scale. I wrote it down. I don't know yet what to do with it.

The special ed testimony landed as confirmation of fears I've been tracking since February. An ed tech at Dyer described his classroom as short-staffed by one to two positions for nearly the entire school year, often with no substitute coverage. A board member described a principal in running shoes sprinting to functional life skills rooms multiple times a day. These are not abstract future risks from a reconfiguration that hasn't happened yet — they are the conditions that exist right now, this year, in the buildings my daughter will eventually enter. The budget that passed 4-2 tonight eliminates two occupational therapist positions from those same classrooms.

The health insurance savings that restored two elementary teachers and two ed techs are real, and I am not dismissing them. But they are one-year-only positions. And the budget has $179,000 in contingency for an entire reconfiguration whose costs are not yet known. Nobody at this meeting can tell me which school Mia attends. That remains the single truest sentence about where things stand.
