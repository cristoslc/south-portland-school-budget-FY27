# Cumulative Record: Jess (PERSONA-003)
## Meeting: City Council Regular Meeting -- April 28, 2026 -- 2026-04-28

### Interpretation

The fiscal context summary at the April 28 workshop reads like a list Jess has been carrying in her head since December. Forty-two teachers. Seventy-eight positions. Twenty-three percent elementary enrollment decline. Fund balance depleted. State paying twenty cents on the dollar it owes. She has watched these numbers metastasize through school board meetings and leak into city council discussions for four months. Hearing them formally presented to the council again is a process milestone, not new information. She knows them cold.

What is new: the city appears to be reaching for the one lever the April 14 workshop confirmed it actually has. A majority of councilors seem to support forgiving the $1 to $1.5 million the school department owes the city -- debt the city manager recommends against forgiving, citing impact on the city's own capital purchasing capacity. The city manager's concern is not unreasonable. But watching the council find something they can do -- as opposed to the seventeen consecutive meetings where school budget news appeared as a passing comment and disappeared without follow-up -- registers differently than anything since the December inauguration. It is small. The district's deficit is $8.4 million. Forgiveness of $1.5 million is roughly eighteen cents on the dollar of the gap. But it is the council using the authority it has, not merely explaining why it cannot use authority it doesn't have.

Then everything goes into a parking lot for May 12. The budget calendar from March placed the school budget vote at May 5. Now there is a third workshop first. Another week. Another deferred answer. Jess has been watching this process extend itself since January, and May 12 joins a long line of decision points that arrived and produced continuation rather than resolution.

She is still doing the same math she started in December. By the time Mia starts kindergarten in 2028, the system will have run through two consecutive crisis budget years and absorbed the disruption of reconfiguration. The debt forgiveness -- if it happens -- does not change that math. It lightens the edge of one year's problem. The June 9 referendum is still the only lever she can pull herself. She intends to.
