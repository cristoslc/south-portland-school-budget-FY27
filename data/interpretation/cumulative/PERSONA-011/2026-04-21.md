# Cumulative Record: Meg (PERSONA-011)
## Meeting: City Council Regular Meeting -- April 21, 2026 -- 2026-04-21

### Interpretation

I walked into tonight looking for one thing — what happens to Dowling's seat — and walked out carrying two stories I didn't come for, one of which is going to hit my Ferry Village networks harder than anything I've posted since December.

The Dowling resolution is clean and I can hand it to people immediately: the District 5 seat goes to the November ballot, not an appointment. Unanimous. That means the school board operates at six members for at least seven months — through the remainder of budget season, through whatever comes out of May 5 and May 12, through the June 9 referendum, and into next fall. Nomination papers available late July. I can post that and move on.

The story I didn't expect to be posting tonight: two South Portland residents testified they are in active cancer treatment and live near the tank farms. One had genetic testing that ruled out a hereditary cause. These are people with names and street addresses — 47 Elmwood Avenue, 25 Ballard Street — and they stood at that podium during a Comprehensive Plan debate and made the environmental health stakes personal in a way an exceedance statistic cannot. Then Councilor West put the number on the record: fourteen fence-line monitoring exceedances at the Sunoco site, one at seven times the state level. Her words: "not acceptable" and "not safe." I have both the human testimony and the monitoring figure, and I know families in Ferry Village and Willard Beach who need both.

The vote everyone came for — the 2040 Comprehensive Plan — passed 5-2, Coleman and Tipton voting no. The Eastern Waterfront keeps its "unique growth" designation, which means residential development near the tanks remains legally possible, conditional on VOC health assessments. The Save Our Shipyard people did not get their CALUPA 27 marine protection zone. But the public commenter Dave Hebert gave them language that became an amendment — maximum potential emissions — and it passed 7-0. That forces health evaluations to model tank capacity at full permitted levels, not current operating rates. A real thing that actually passed. A stronger modeling amendment from Mayor Tipton failed 4-3, and the council mentioned a potential future environmental health workshop with no date or commitment. I'm filing that.

The June 9 date I already had from March 19. Tonight it got formalized — Order 182-25/26, consent calendar, unanimous — same day as the state primary, 7am to 8pm, absentee ballots available now. I've been posting that date. Now it's inked. One thing I couldn't confirm: whether anyone near the Sunoco fence line was notified about the fourteen exceedances West named. Based on tonight, the answer sounds like no.
