# Cumulative Record: Meg (PERSONA-011)
## Meeting: School Board Regular Meeting -- April 13, 2026 -- 2026-04-13

### Interpretation

The lead landed in the first three minutes. Chair DeAngelis opened the meeting by announcing the board will vote April 29 to halt September 2026 reconfiguration. I'm leading with it, and I'm leading with it carefully — it's not final until April 29, and she was explicit that the model itself is not being abandoned. Both members who previously opposed reconfiguration have committed to the plan on a later timeline. But for weeks I've been telling my group chats that Option A is happening this September — Dyer and Small going PreK–1, Brown and Skillin going grades 2–4, Kaylor closed, kids moving in August. Tonight that whole September timeline got paused. April 29 is on every calendar I have.

The story that will hit my local group chats harder than any vote: Beth Kellogg, Brown Elementary principal, is resigning effective June 30. Two classroom teachers are also leaving — a Skillin special ed teacher and a Dyer first grade teacher — and those might be filled from the recall list. The principal won't be. Brown families needed to hear that tonight and most of them weren't watching.

The finance director was asked directly in public comment whether Kaylor could still be kept open, and she said on the record: "I really don't think so... I can't recommend that move financially." She also put the number on it: the Kaylor closure accounts for roughly $4 million of the total reductions — about half of all cuts. That's the clearest, most citable summary of why that building is driving so much of this budget, and I've been waiting for it for weeks.

What I cannot hand people cleanly: the current-year deficit is somewhere between $80,000 and $1.1 million. The fund balance is already negative. And there's an unknown SPTA time-beyond-contract liability — submissions still being collected, no figure until May. The board voted to ask the city for $360K in shared service offsets and a 1% tax increase into reserves. Not into positions. Someone tried to motion 0.5% for position restoration. Couldn't get a second. I'm reporting that.

The embedded OT question I flagged after April 7 now has an answer from Director Cox: the model changes. The OTs working directly inside life skills classrooms at Dyer and Skillin are gone. The district returns to pull-out and consultative service — 3.8 OTs for 138 students. That's a 1:36 ratio. Each elementary school gets a half-time BCBA and half-time instructional strategist. For families with kids in those classrooms, the daily routine is changing and no one has spelled out what that looks like yet.

The finance director also said FY28 will require careful planning to stay under 6%. A Dyer teacher asked whether next March would look like this March. No definitive answer. My answer: I'll be watching.
