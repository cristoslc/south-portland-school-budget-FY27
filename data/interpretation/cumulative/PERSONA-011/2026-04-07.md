# Cumulative Record: Meg (PERSONA-011)
## Meeting: School Board Regular Meeting -- April 7, 2026 -- 2026-04-07

### Interpretation

After five months of watching this district drag its budget crisis through meeting after meeting, I finally have a passed budget to report. Four months since the spending freeze, six weeks since the March 2 workshop where they presented nine reconfiguration models, two failed votes and one adjournment at 11pm — and tonight the board voted 4-2 to send FY27 to city council. Holman, Smith, DeAngelis, Risch yes; Feller and Richardson no. I have the names. I'm posting them.

The lead I'm actually going with: Vice Chair Dowling resigned, and we found out because the Portland Press Herald published it while the meeting was still running. A parent read the article out loud during second public comment. The chair had said earlier that Dowling "wasn't able to be present tonight." That's it. No explanation to the community, no heads up, nothing. I've been frustrated before when the district was slow with information — the January 15 goal-setting workshop where I had zero from the room, the reconfiguration slides that didn't drop until the Friday before the March 23 meeting. This is different. This is a governing body losing its vice chair in the middle of one of the most consequential budget seasons in recent memory, and the community found out via a phone alert in the audience. I'm reporting what happened without speculating about why, but I'm also not pretending it looks anything other than what it looks like.

The good news I can hand people: the district found $655,000 in health insurance savings — actual cost came in at 5.14% instead of the 11.5% cap I've been reporting since March 30. That money bought back four positions: two elementary classroom teachers, two elementary special education ed techs. All four are one-year only, grade and school assignments still TBD. There's also $302,000 in unconfirmed state emergency funds that could add a behavior analyst and a middle school related arts teacher, also one-year only. I'm flagging those as unconfirmed because that's what they are.

What I cannot hand people without a note of alarm: Dr. Prince was asked directly what the plan is for replacing the two OTs being cut from functional life skills classrooms. His answer on the record: "I don't think there is a specific plan right now." Those replacement ed tech positions aren't filled. If you have a kid in an FLS classroom, you need to know that August is coming and there is currently no named plan for who is in that room.

The SPTA survey result — 92% negative morale, 76% opposing the budget — is the number that will circulate widest in my group chats. I'm including it because it's documented and it's real. And a recall petition against DeAngelis was filed tonight by a parent at the podium. I'll report it, but I'm not amplifying it beyond what was stated. The April 14 council presentation is next, and the chair said the budget is not in final form. There's still movement to track.
