# Cumulative Record: Meg (PERSONA-011)
## Meeting: City Council Regular Meeting -- April 28, 2026 -- 2026-04-28

### Interpretation

This is what the city council budget process looks like from the inside: the school district — the entity consuming 61 cents of every property tax dollar, the crisis I've been relaying for five months — is item nine of twelve tonight. I sat through ten other department presentations before the school segment arrived, on my phone, waiting. That's not a complaint. That's what this is. But after five months of the school budget dominating every conversation in my networks, there's something clarifying about watching it get its ten to fifteen allocated minutes alongside snow removal contracts and parking management plans.

Here's what those minutes produced. The fund balance forgiveness number is better than feared. The city had been planning around $3 million in school FY26 overage it might need to absorb. The school is now saying $1 to $1.5 million — roughly half of what the city reserved. That's the first time in a long time the actual number came in below the scary estimate, and I'm noting it because my group chats deserve to hear that occasionally.

The complication: the city manager's official written recommendation is to forgive nothing. Zero. His reasoning is direct — whatever the city forgives either eats into capital purchases or forces a city-side tax increase to compensate. Councilors were already on record at the April 14 meeting as being open to some amount of forgiveness, but no number has been named. The school is pressing for a specific figure because whatever it is, or isn't, shapes how they build the FY27 budget. The school board already asked the council for $360,000 in shared service offsets and a 1% contingency increase at the April 13 meeting. Those requests are sitting in the same parking lot heading to May 12.

The parking lot math is the number I'm leading with: if all currently queued items are funded as parked, the tax rate moves from 5.1% to 6.5%. That's a real ceiling before the May 12 sorting begins.

West wasn't there. I've been watching her as the consistent voice in that room for accountability and spending on community needs since December 4th, and she submitted her items in advance through the city manager rather than being present to push during the school segment. Her absence doesn't change the vote count on May 12, but she's been the one most likely to advocate past the script.

Nothing is decided. May 12 is Workshop Three and the final decision point. I'll be there.
