# Cumulative Summary: Meg (PERSONA-011)

### Current Understanding

As of April 28, 2026, Meg understands the South Portland school budget crisis through five months of close observation across both governing bodies. The FY27 proposed budget carries a $7.2 million structural gap — the figure in official council materials, though Councilor Matthews cited $8.4 million on the record in March without a reconciled explanation. Seventy-two layoff notices went out March 18; the official position count remains 78 eliminations, including 42 teachers with 32 on a recall list. Health insurance actual cost came in at 5.14% rather than the 11.5% cap used in the March 30 model, generating roughly $655,000 in savings that restored four elementary positions on a one-year basis. The budget was adopted 4-2 by the board on April 7 and presented to city council on April 14.

Kaler Elementary is authorized for closure (6-1, DeAngelis the sole no vote on equity grounds). Option A reconfiguration — PreK-1 at Dyer and Small, grades 2-4 at Brown and Skillin — was approved, but September 2026 implementation has been halted pending an April 29 board vote; members who previously opposed reconfiguration have committed to the model on a deferred timeline. The 6% tax ceiling is confirmed at approximately $257 per year on a home assessed at the South Portland average of $514,000. No attendance boundaries have been published.

Structural factors driving the crisis include: the state covering roughly 20% of costs against a 55% statutory obligation; elementary enrollment declining 23% while district staffing grew by 82 positions; seven finance directors in six years enabling $400,000 in mismanaged grant funds and repeat legal budget overspending; and TIF structures on major development projects representing long-term diversions of property tax revenue from the general fund.

May 12 is the next critical moment: city council Workshop Three will address fund balance forgiveness (school estimates $1–1.5M in FY26 overage, below the $3M the city reserved; city manager recommends zero forgiveness), the $360,000 shared services offset request, and a 1% contingency ask. The June 9 voter referendum is the ultimate public decision point. The finance director has warned that FY28 will be structurally harder than FY27.
