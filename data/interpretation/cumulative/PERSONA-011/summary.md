- **Will the Mahoney-only scaled-back option produce a number low enough to be viable alongside a school tax increase?** Six scenarios ranging from $57M to $104M were presented at the February 17 workshop; the Mahoney committee recommended against the bare-bones approach; no formal vote was reached at that session. (resolved 2026-02-17)

- **Will schools close?** No school closures were announced at the February 4th forum; the district stated buildings are needed to accommodate state-mandated pre-K expansion. (resolved 2026-02-04; subsequently superseded when Kaler closure was authorized March 30)

- **Council tax guidance range?** Council guidance landed at 4–6% for the school tax increase, with most members gravitating toward the higher end. (resolved 2026-02-10)

- **With Project HOME projected to run out of funds by approximately February 27 and the eviction moratorium defeated, what happens to the pipeline of families awaiting emergency rental assistance?** A $100,000 city reimbursement to Project HOME passed 6-1 at the March 19 council meeting, providing a bridge beyond the projected funding exhaustion date. (resolved 2026-03-19)

- **How does the Mahoney project decision timeline interact with school budget finalization — will South Portland taxpayers be asked to absorb both a school tax increase and a capital bond in the same cycle?** The Mahoney City Center November 2026 referendum was definitively abandoned at the March 19 council meeting; the combined 2026 burden scenario does not materialize. (resolved 2026-03-19)

- **Will the approximately $100,000 Project HOME reimbursement order hold when it comes to a council vote?** Passed 6-1 at the March 19 council meeting, with Councilor Matthews as the sole no vote. (resolved 2026-03-19)

- **Which refined reconfiguration option will survive now that Skillin is protected?** Option A selected: PreK–1 at Dyer and Small; grades 2–4 at Brown and Skillin; Kaler closed. (resolved 2026-03-30)

- **Will the board formally move the tax ceiling above 6%?** The FY27 budget is proposed at exactly 6%; household impact is $257/year for the South Portland average home. No board member moved to change the tax figure; the failed budget votes turned on specific positions, not the rate. (resolved 2026-03-23, confirmed 2026-03-30)

- **Will the board vote at March 30 on Options A and B and the school closure?** The reconfiguration and closure votes both passed (Option A approximately 5-2; Kaler closure 6-1); the FY27 budget itself failed 5-2 and required a further meeting. (resolved 2026-03-30)

- **Will the four early release days proposed for reconfiguration preparation be adopted?** Removed unanimously; a state waiver for one fewer student day is being pursued as a partial alternative. (resolved 2026-04-02)

- **Who absorbs the 40-plus active behavior plans held by the elementary behavioral strategist if that position is eliminated?** The district is returning to a pull-out and consultative OT service model; each elementary school receives a half-time BCBA and half-time instructional strategist. (resolved 2026-04-13)

- **Does the board adopt the budget before the April 7 council presentation?** Budget passed 4-2 (Holman, Smith, DeAngelis, Risch yes; Feller and Richardson no); council presentation proceeded with an adopted proposal. (resolved 2026-04-07)

- **What specific changes to the DEI director role, behavior ed specialist, and related arts positions will be made before the April 2 deadline?** Health insurance savings restored four elementary positions (two classroom teachers, two special education ed techs), all one-year only; the BCBA role is proposed pending unconfirmed state funds; the DEI-to-SPTA-strategist conversion that DeAngelis objected to on March 30 remains in the budget as adopted. (resolved 2026-04-07)

- **If state funding materializes, what process does the board use to decide which eliminated positions are restored?** The administration identified specific intended uses ($302K toward one BCBA and one related arts teacher, both one-year only), but the funds remain unconfirmed and no formal allocation process has been established for any amounts beyond that. (resolved 2026-04-07, partially)

- **Is September 2026 implementation realistic given the pace of decision-making?** No; the board is voting April 29 to officially halt September 2026 implementation; the reconfiguration model survives on a deferred timeline. (resolved 2026-04-13)

- **What is the specific plan for replacing the two OTs in functional life skills classrooms before August?** The district is returning to a pull-out and consultative service model: 3.8 OTs projected for 138 students (1:36 ratio); each elementary school receives a half-time BCBA and half-time instructional strategist. Individual student transition plans have not been published. (resolved 2026-04-13)
