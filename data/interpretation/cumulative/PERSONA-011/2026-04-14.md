# Cumulative Record: Meg (PERSONA-011)
## Meeting: City Council Regular Meeting -- April 14, 2026 -- 2026-04-14

### Interpretation

Tonight was old news delivered officially, and that's a strange thing to sit with after five months of being the person who always had it first.

The $7.2 million gap, the 78 positions, the 23% elementary enrollment drop, the state covering 20% when it owes 55%, the per-pupil cost of $26,651 — I've been reporting every one of those numbers in my group chats since December. Tonight the council finally received them in a formal presentation. I'm not complaining. The process is the process. But I've been living four months ahead of this particular meeting, and that dissonance is real.

The delay itself needs a quick explanation for anyone who tried to tune in on April 7: the school board's budget adoption and the council's scheduled public hearing fell on the same night. The board passed the budget 4-2 that night, but you can't present a budget to the council that technically didn't exist yet when the hearing started. So it moved a week. Now it's official.

The piece I'm actually leading with tonight is one my group chats needed and didn't have: the council cannot save your kid's school. Not your kid's teacher, not the percussion ed tech, not Kayler, not anything specific. State law limits the council to voting on the total dollar amount — up, down, or pass-through. That's the full extent of their authority over the school budget. Parents have been emailing councilors asking them to protect things. I understand it. But that's not how the law works, and the city manager's position paper makes it explicit. Better to know now than to find out in May when the vote happens and nothing specific changed.

The one number I'm watching carefully: tonight's official materials use $7.2M as the structural gap. When Matthews put $8.4M on the record March 19, I flagged it and couldn't resolve the discrepancy. The official council presentation uses $7.2M. I'm not declaring the higher number wrong — the source still isn't clear to me — but for now, $7.2M is what's in front of the council.

June 9 is the next date. Council votes May 5 on the total amount. Then it goes to voters. That's the one that matters.
