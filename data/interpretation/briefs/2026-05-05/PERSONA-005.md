## Open Questions

- **Where do the 42 teacher and 16 ed tech cuts actually land?** I still have no school-by-school breakdown. Are the cuts concentrated at the schools with the highest percentages of multilingual learners, IEP students, and students experiencing poverty — or is the administration distributing them in a way that holds up to scrutiny? I can't evaluate this without the data, and the data has not been provided.

- **Has anyone produced a Title VI analysis before Kayler's closure is finalized?** This question went unanswered at the March 23 adjournment. The Finance Director called the closure financially irreversible in April. So either the analysis was done and not shared, it was started and is incomplete, or it hasn't been started. Which is it?

- **Where did the $300,000 in state funds actually go?** The administration proposed routing it to contingency and one-year positions, and the 3-2 board vote sent potential cost offsets to contingency over position restoration. But that was a budget vote — have the funds actually been allocated, and to what specifically? Is there any written accounting of how these dollars, which the union specifically secured for low-income and unhoused students, are being used?

- **What happened to Action 20A and Action 20B?** The board mandated an attendance zone analysis with an outside consultant (due January 2025) and annual demographic reporting (due November 2024). Neither has appeared in the meeting record through April 14. I'm not going to pretend those deadlines slipped without anyone noticing. Who owns these mandates and when will they be delivered?

- **Will the eight dimensions of disaggregated data requested at the April 2 meeting ever be produced?** Chronic absenteeism, grade retention, gifted enrollment, enrichment access, discipline rates, safety perceptions, bullying incidents, counselor ratios — all broken down by race, income, disability, and English learner status. That was a specific, on-the-record request. What's the answer?

- **What happens to the DEI function when the Maine DOE Community Schools grant runs out?** The position was demoted to instructional strategist, salary contingent on a grant with no stated renewal pathway. There is no independent equity oversight with administrative authority in the FY27 budget. Does the council understand what they are approving?

- **Is any councilor going to require equity-disaggregated impact data as a condition of budget approval?** Or is this hearing going to proceed the same way the board did — with equity language in the preamble and no equity analysis in the substance?

- **Are multilingual families and families of students with IEPs going to have real access to the May 5 hearing?** Or will translation services, accessible formats, and outreach to the Red Bank community be an afterthought again?

- **Will Holman's point about non-citizen parents unable to vote in the June 9 referendum get any structural response?** The families most directly affected by Kayler's closure may be the least able to participate in the referendum meant to hold the district accountable for the decision. What is being done about that?

## Agenda Implications

No agenda has been published for the May 5 meeting. Based on the cumulative record and the position of this meeting in the budget calendar, the following topics are likely and Priya should prepare for each.

**The city council public hearing on the school budget** is the defined purpose of this meeting. This is one of two remaining public accountability windows before the June 9 referendum. It is the moment when public comment formally enters the council's deliberative record on the FY27 school budget.

*What this means for Priya specifically:* This is the last structured opportunity to place a demand for disaggregated equity data into the council's official record before voters are asked to approve or reject the budget. If the council approves the budget without ever requiring a school-by-school breakdown of cuts, that failure becomes part of the public record too — and that record matters for future advocacy, public records requests, and any Title VI complaint process.

*What to listen for:* Whether any councilor raises the absence of equity-disaggregated impact data as a substantive concern, not just a procedural footnote. Whether the council asks the administration directly which schools absorb the 42 teacher and 16 ed tech eliminations, and whether responses are specific or gestural. Whether the Kayler closure is treated as settled (because the Finance Director confirmed it is financially irreversible) or whether any councilor attempts to reopen it. Whether the DEI Director demotion is named at the council level at all.

*Questions to prepare:*
- A direct, brief public comment asking the council to name, before the vote, which schools receive which cuts — by school, by position type, by whether the eliminated staff hold ELL endorsements or special education credentials. Frame this as a condition for informed consent, not an accusation.
- A specific question about Title VI: has the city's legal counsel reviewed the Kayler closure for civil rights compliance, and if not, why not?
- A question about the $300,000 in state funds: can the administration state, on the record at this hearing, what those dollars are specifically funding in FY27?

**The reconfiguration pause motion outcome (April 29)** will shape what is politically live at the May 5 hearing. If the pause passed in a form that reopens any aspect of the reconfiguration, there may be an opening to introduce equity conditions — translated engagement materials, demographic transition planning, bus stop mapping — into the pause terms. If the pause was narrow or failed, Priya's leverage shifts entirely to the referendum and the public record she builds for post-June 9 accountability.

*What to listen for:* Whether board members or the superintendent reference the April 29 outcome in their presentation to the council, and how they characterize the reconfiguration timeline. Whether "pause" is being treated as procedural delay or as a genuine reopening.

**The referendum framing** will almost certainly surface during or around this hearing, as June 9 is seven weeks out. Listen for whether the public-facing framing presents the referendum as a simple yes/no on tax rate, or whether any official will explain what a no vote means for specific programs and populations. The state funding story — 20% actual coverage against a 55% statutory obligation — needs to be in the public record in plain language before June 9. This hearing is the best remaining opportunity for that to happen.
