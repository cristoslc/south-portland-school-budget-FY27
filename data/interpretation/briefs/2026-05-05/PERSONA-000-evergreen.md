# General Budget Briefing
## Current Budget Picture

## Budget Snapshot

South Portland's proposed school budget for the fiscal year beginning July 1, 2026 (FY27) stands at approximately **$75.8 million** for K–12 programs, plus $250,000 for Adult Education. That total reflects a local tax increase of just under **6%** — the target the school board set as its ceiling earlier in the budget cycle.

To reach that figure, the district cut roughly **$8.4 million** from what it would have cost to maintain current programs and staffing. Those reductions include the closure of **Kaylor Elementary School**, which the Finance Director estimates accounts for roughly **$4 million**, or about half of the total savings. The rest came from eliminating positions and reducing spending across the district. Approximately **74 staff positions** were cut — about 12% of the workforce.

The school board adopted this budget on **April 7, 2026** by a 4–2 vote and presented it to the City Council at a workshop on **April 14, 2026**. Before that council workshop, the board voted on two requests to the council: (1) ask the city to offset $360,000 in shared service costs — specifically School Resource Officers (estimated at $220,000) and snow removal and sanding (estimated at $140,000); and (2) ask the city to approve a **1% additional tax increase** to put money into the district's contingency fund, which would help rebuild a depleted fund balance. Both requests passed.

The budget is not yet final. The City Council holds a public hearing and vote on **May 5, 2026** to determine what amount it will send to voters. If the council acts, residents will vote on the school budget at a **validation referendum on June 9, 2026**.

## What Changed

Several significant developments occurred in the weeks covered by these meetings:

**Health insurance savings freed up funds.** The district had been budgeting based on an assumed 11.5% health insurance increase. The actual increase came in at **5.14%**, freeing approximately **$655,000**. The board directed a portion of those savings toward contingency funds, two classroom teaching positions (posted as one-year-only), two special education ed techs, and dollars for ongoing contract discussions with two employee units.

**Reconfiguration of elementary schools was delayed.** Earlier meetings had included a plan to reorganize which grade levels are taught at which schools, starting in September 2026. On April 13, the board chair announced that the board intends to **halt that reconfiguration for fall 2026** and move the target to **fall 2027**, citing public concern and the need for more planning time. A formal vote on that delay was scheduled for a special meeting on **April 29, 2026**. The school closure of Kaylor remains in place.

**The Finance Director presented a location-based budget breakdown.** For the first time, staff sorted the budget by school building rather than by cost category. That analysis showed that closing Kaylor represents closer to a **$4 million impact** on the budget — not the approximately $400,000 in facilities savings that had been cited previously and caused some confusion. The larger figure accounts for staffing and all costs allocated to that building.

**The board's Vice Chair resigned.** Member Adrian Dowling resigned from the board. Member Tyler Smith was elected Vice Chair at the April 13 meeting.

**The current fiscal year (FY26) may end in deficit.** The Finance Director reported that the district is projecting a deficit in the current year in the range of **$80,000 to $1.1 million** — a wide range because several costs remain unresolved, including time-beyond-contract payments owed to teachers under a recently ratified contract. That deficit would draw on city reserves and require repayment.

## Major Decisions Ahead

**City Council action on the school budget (May 5, 2026).** The council holds a public hearing and then decides whether to accept the school board's proposed budget, increase it, or decrease it. Under state law and the city charter, the council can only act on the bottom-line number — it cannot direct the district on which specific items to cut or restore. If the council changes the total, the school board must come back with a revised plan. The council votes at this hearing to send the budget to voters for the June 9 referendum.

**Validation referendum (June 9, 2026).** Registered voters in South Portland will cast ballots to approve or reject the school budget.

**April 29 special board meeting.** The board was scheduled to vote formally on halting the elementary school reconfiguration for this fall. The outcome of that vote determines what, if any, structural school changes take effect in September 2026 beyond the Kaylor closure.

**Recall and rehiring decisions.** The district had issued layoff notices to approximately 74 staff. As the budget process concludes, the board and administration need to determine which, if any, additional positions can be restored — and through what process. Staff on the "recall list" have recall rights under their contracts.

**FY26 deficit resolution.** The board and city need to reach an agreement on how the current-year deficit will be covered and repaid. The exact amount of the deficit remains unresolved as of the meetings reviewed here.

## Open Questions

- **What is the exact FY26 deficit?** The projected range is $80,000 to $1.1 million, but the Finance Director noted that time-beyond-contract payments owed to teachers under the SPTA contract have not yet been quantified and will add to that number. The full amount will not be known until the fiscal year closes.

- **Will the city council accept the board's two requests?** The board asked the council to (a) offset $360,000 in shared services and (b) allow a 1% tax increase to seed the contingency fund. Neither request had been formally responded to by the council as of the April 14 workshop.

- **Will additional state funding materialize?** The district was told Augusta may allocate approximately $152,000 for students from lower-income households and $150,000 for homeless youth. As of April 13, those dollars were not confirmed in the state allocation.

- **How will special education staffing gaps be filled?** The district is moving away from an embedded occupational therapist model in two functional life skills classrooms and plans to hire ed techs instead. The number of special education ed techs needed for FY27 depends on IEP counts that are still being finalized, including incoming students from Child Development Services (CDS).

- **What will the Kaylor building be used for?** The district intends to retain the building rather than sell it. No confirmed plan for the building — lease, future school use, or other purpose — was established in the meetings reviewed here.

- **What does the reconfiguration delay mean for Kaylor students and staff?** The board announced it would halt reconfiguration for fall 2026, but as of April 13 had not yet voted on that formally or presented a plan for how Kaylor students and staff will transition through the end of the school year and into fall placements.

- **What are the full costs of reconfiguration for fall 2027?** The district has budgeted $75,000 for moving costs and approximately $179,000 in contingency. Critics at public meetings argued those figures do not fully capture transportation changes, building modifications, and planning time. No comprehensive cost estimate was presented.

- **What will FY28 look like?** The Finance Director stated that early modeling suggests the district will need "careful planning" to stay under 6% the following year, but named significant unknowns including health insurance rates, the updated Essential Programs and Services (EPS) state funding formula, health insurance, and broader economic conditions.
